### [EmbodiedEvo: Self-Evolving Coding Agent For Physical Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18867v2)

- **Authors:** Wenyi Li, Yizhe Chi, Youjie Zheng, Chunyu Liu, Xiaoqiu Wang, Mingju Gao, Zheng Jiang, Deyao Hong, Calvin Xiao, Kaisen Yang, Qinhuai Na
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18867v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18867v2)

Coding agents can construct executable programs and revise them through feedback, offering a foundation for embodied control without target-task fine-tuning. Through a programmatic robot interface, the agent observes the scene, executes control programs, and adapts them to the resulting physical state, serving as the closed-loop policy. However, this within-episode adaptation does not automatically preserve experience across scenes. Our trajectory analysis shows that agents spend interaction and inference budgets establishing effective operations and identifying their preconditions, while fresh agents cannot reuse these discoveries. This motivates EmbodiedEvo, a self-evolving coding agent that distills physical feedback into reusable code Skills and diagnostic Lessons. Experience is acquired on one Playground scene per task, then frozen for independent agents in unseen scenes, without updating model weights. On RoboDojo-Sim, EmbodiedEvo achieves an average score of 44.48 and a success rate of 37.82\%, surpassing all officially verified results. Paired tests show the largest gains on memory-dependent tasks and successful reuse of coordinated procedures, while fine contact control remains difficult. Experience from stronger models also improves weaker executors: Astra's experience raises Sol and Luna's success rates by 8.86 and 5.50 percentage points over no experience. Compared with raw interaction history, Skills and Lessons achieve comparable task performance at 53.0\% lower execution cost.

### [Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868v1)

- **Authors:** Wenbo Hu, Shraman Pramanick, Krishna Kumar Singh, Nanyun Peng, Kai-Wei Chang, Yong Jae Lee
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18868v1)

Long-horizon agents have drawn growing attention for their ability to work autonomously -- from hours to days -- on economically valuable tasks, a capability essential to automated discovery and recursive self-improvement (RSI). Yet open-source models still struggle on long-horizon tasks, largely for lack of both training data and a working recipe, and scaling both is hard: long, complex tasks are difficult to curate and verify, and their rewards are sparse. We address both. First, we introduce Tev-Gym, the first long-horizon agent training dataset with verifiable, dense, progressive rewards -- 1,170 verified task environments across 7 domains. It is built on a latent-world-first approach that first constructs a latent world: a complete and correct hidden specification of the task domain, workflow, and expected state after each step. Each task is then progressively rendered from this specification into an agent-facing environment, enabling controllable complexity and a natural progress metric. Second, we turn this metric into a training recipe: via progress-potential reward shaping (PPRS), we convert progress into a dense per-step reward that improves training efficiency and makes otherwise-intractable tasks trainable. Training with this recipe yields Tev-27B, which achieves substantial gains on the Agents' Last Exam computing and math sciences domains (ALE-CM), matching GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 at Pass@5. Despite training only on ALE-related data, it generalizes to the Terminal-Bench (TB) 3.0 and 4.0 subsets, surpassing GLM 5.2 and Gemini 3.7 Flash, and reaches Claude Opus 4.8-level performance on middle-horizon tasks such as TB 2.1.

### [The Last Remote Sensing Model Built by Humans? Casting Any Earth Observation Task as Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18845v2)

- **Authors:** Sijie Zhao, Zeqi Zhou, Heli Qi, Xuanchao Fu, Zhuo Zheng, Junshi Xia, Junjue Wang, Weihao Xuan, Naoto Yokoya
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18845v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18845v2)

Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand. For each new task, experts design a training recipe and tune it against a benchmark. We show that this loop can be handed to an agent. Any verifiable task can be cast as a harness and a verifier, where the harness defines what the agent may use and the verifier defines what counts as success. Every EO task can be made verifiable, through labeled benchmarks, independent sensors, physical models or the repeated observation of the same place. Given only these two components, a general-purpose coding agent builds the EO model and improves it recursively on its own outputs. Settings long treated as separate research problems, from learning with a handful of labels to adapting across domains, become choices of harness and verifier. On six EO benchmarks spanning optical, SAR and multispectral time-series imagery, with outputs that range from land-cover maps and ship instances to canopy heights and answers about large images, agent-built models surpass the state of the art on every one, and on land-cover mapping, they do so from 11% of the training labels. This establishes a new paradigm for EO, in which people design harnesses and verifiers and agents build the models, so EO models improve as agents do.

### [Self-Evolving Agent Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18813v1)

- **Authors:** Chuyang Wei, Haoxiang Guan, Minghang Zhu, Kexin Zhou, Jiyan He, Xiren Zhou, Huanhuan Chen, Yifei Shen
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18813v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18813v1)

Harness self-evolution revises an agent's software scaffolding, including prompts, memory, workflows, and executable components, while keeping model weights fixed. We formalize a feedback-driven process in which accepted revisions persist across runs. The framework separates update targets from proposal and admission mechanisms. It distinguishes changes to interpreted state from revisions to tools and code, repository-level control logic, and plugin composition. We characterize search through evolutionary optimization and examine its reliability through continual PAC-learning. Under explicit assumptions, the analysis yields an expected generalization bound, a retention bound conditional on historical regression budgets, and a high-probability admission rule using fresh, independent validation data. We examine how persistent harness revisions affect knowledge sharing and organizational workflows, with implications for scientific reliability, physical safety, and institutional accountability. Cases from prior work illustrate model--harness co-design, distributed execution, and plugin reuse. Reliable deployment requires continual validation as task distributions and evaluators change, with independent safeguards for learning, runtime privileges, and consequential actions. We provide a comprehensive 66-page discussion of related work, theory, algorithms, evaluation, applications, and the broader impact of Harness RSI, together with continuously updated code and supplementary materials, at https://github.com/zgcagi/Harness-RSI.

### [SEAR: Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18792v1)

- **Authors:** Xinyi Wang, Bangzheng Li, Hanyu Wang, Lin Zhang, Andrea Yaoyun Cui, Zhenggang Tang, Shyamal Buch, Dejia Xu
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18792v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18792v1)

Frontier language-model agents can now improve robots on their own: they write control code, test it, and keep what works as skills and memories for the next task. We introduce SEAR (Self-Evolving Agentic Robotics), a framework and benchmark built around two questions. Is the agent really learning, or just trying more often? And does what it learns still help on new tasks, in a new simulator, and on a real robot? The framework runs an agent in three modes: it writes code that becomes the policy (code-as-policy), acts live in the control loop (agent-as-policy), or works as a researcher that fine-tunes a vision-language-action model (auto-research). The benchmark adds controls that separate retained experience from repeated attempts, familiar tasks, privileged simulator state, larger budgets, and luck. Across seven frontier language models, 330 simulated and five physical tasks, and over four years of agent runtime, we highlight three findings. First, the learning curve of a robotic agent depends both on the overall time budget and the task difficulty relative to model capability. Harder tasks make longer sessions worthwhile; on easier tasks, re-rolling short attempts does just as well. Second, control code and shared routines transfer across tasks and simulators, while task-specific solutions do not. On a real robot, we observe a performance gap between agent-designed practice and novel human-designed scenes. Third, different models evolve more effectively in different ways. Some do best acting live, others writing code first. And a text-only model keeps pace with a comparable multimodal one: perception helps with error assessment, but agentic reasoning and tool use can matter more. We release the benchmark and codebase to support future research.

### [RSIgen: Reciprocal Self-Improvement for Agentic Visual Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18688v2)

- **Authors:** Yidi Liu, Haozhe Wang, Jie Xiao, Kai Zhu, Xueyang Fu, Zheng-Jun Zha
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18688v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18688v2)

Agentic visual generation produces experience that can improve the native understanding (agent) and generation roles of a unified multimodal backbone, but task success alone does not determine which role should learn. Generator updates can also make the agent's action supervision stale. We introduce RSIgen, a task-centric reciprocal self-improvement framework linking learning allocation, supervision refresh, and recursive curriculum construction. Dynamic Responsibility Routing (DRR) uses matched interventions on evidence, inputs, and supported actions to select learning recipients and construct compatible training targets. After generator learning, RSIgen re-evaluates the relative utility of tested actions with branch inputs held fixed to refresh agent supervision; the retained roles then construct the next curriculum. Across BAGEL, Qwen-Image, and Wan-Image-7B, RSIgen improves AgentGen-Bench Full Overall9 by 3.2--3.3 points over a three-cycle SearchGen adaptation with action costs. Wan controls support allocation gains at matched role-level training exposure, refresh gains at a matched adaptation budget, and benefits from updated curriculum sources while recipients continue learning. In blind evaluation on 200 real-user requests, pooled tie-adjusted preference scores against the same baseline reach 70.0% for task completion and 64.9% for visual quality.

### [ProAS: A Progress-Aware Advantage Shaping Framework for Multi-Turn Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18683v1)

- **Authors:** Bowen Ding, Mingda Wan, Yuhan Chen, Jiyao Yuan, Qi Zhu, Futing Wang, Heyuan Deng, Fei Mi, Lifeng Shang, Tao Lin
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18683v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18683v1)

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as the primary paradigm for optimizing Large Language Model (LLM) agents, yet efficient critic-free algorithms (e.g., GRPO) suffer from a fundamental uniform credit assignment dilemma that broadcasts trajectory-level advantages equally to all actions. Our analysis reveals that this uniformity inherently reinforces structurally invalid actions in preferred trajectories and ignores the downstream cascading influence of early pivotal deviations in unpreferred ones. To resolve this without introducing critic overhead, we propose Progress-Aware Advantage Shaping (ProAS), a plug-and-play framework that leverages a dynamically evolving Agentic Reference Graph (ARG) to achieve fine-grained credit assignment via Masking Unreasonable Incentives (MUI) and Asymmetric Penalty Redistribution (APR). Extensive experiments across Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct demonstrate that ProAS universally elevates mainstream group-based baselines to new state-of-the-art performance, achieving peak success rates of 91.9\% and 94.3\% on ALFWorld, alongside 66.7\% and 70.8\% on WebShop, respectively. Furthermore, it accelerates training convergence by up to $2.44\times$ while maintaining strong robustness against imperfect or distilled ARGs. Codes: https://github.com/LINs-lab/ProAS.

### [ReTerm: Recovery Training for Self-Improving Terminal Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18484v2)

- **Authors:** Wei Xie, Kaituo Feng, Shiming Fu, Yong Liu, Xiangyu Yue, Shuangjia Zheng
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.SE
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18484v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18484v2)

Terminal agents have shown strong abilities in complex coding tasks. However, they often struggle to recover from errors during long-running execution (e.g., command errors, faulty implementations), eventually leading to task failures. To this end, we present ReTerm, a self-improving terminal agent trained by learning how to recover from its own failed terminal tasks. To support its training, we construct a customized data pipeline and produce ReTerm-10K, a high-quality dataset that provides effective recovery demonstrations for SFT cold start. Furthermore, we propose ReTerm-GRPO, an online RL algorithm that forks from the agent's own failure states to generate multiple independent self-recovery attempts. By learning from whether these attempts successfully complete the task, the agent self-improves through its own recovery experience. Built on Qwen3.5-9B, our ReTerm-9B achieves 34.8% Pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, improving over the base model by 14.6 points. Further analysis shows that our method achieves consistently higher recovery rates across diverse error categories compared with standard GRPO training. All code, data and model are released at https://github.com/twothsnd/ReTerm.

### [ABot-ER: Unified Embodied Reasoning through Agentic Data-Model Co-Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18355v1)

- **Authors:** Qiuyu Ding, Heng-Da Xu, Changda Xia, Jiajun Shen, Zihao Guan, Zhengchen Yuan, Jianfang Zhang, Di Yang, Jiayi Tian, Yuting Xu, Fei Wang, Shiao Liu, Wei Zhang, Xiaolong Wu, Wenbin Tang, Zedong Chu, Mu Xu
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18355v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18355v1)

We introduce \textbf{ABot Embodied Reasoning (ABot-ER)}, a unified embodied reasoning foundation model that breaks the tension between breadth and depth in embodied reasoning. Embodied intelligence demands the joint mastery of perception, planning, and execution, yet reconciling generality with specialization across this spectrum remains an open challenge. Within a single unified model, ABot-ER commands specialist-level depth across all three competencies, spanning spatial, scene, and temporal reasoning in perception; long-horizon action organization in planning; and point grounding and affordance in execution. The \textbf{Agentic Data-Model Self-Evolution Engine} continuously adapts data strategies to the model's evolving capability state. The \textbf{Task-Conditioned Adaptive Training Strategy} uses task tokens to activate lightweight low-rank residuals over a shared backbone, separating updates from heterogeneous output formats while retaining shared embodied knowledge. Across a systematic evaluation on twelve benchmarks covering three core competencies and five capability dimensions, ABot-ER achieves \textbf{state-of-the-art overall performance} across perception, planning, and execution within a unified parametric framework, demonstrating that breadth and depth can coexist in unified embodied reasoning.

### [APT: Feedback-Driven Agentic Post-Training for Text-to-Image Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18097v1)

- **Authors:** Sirui Zhang, Fangyun Wei, Dong Chen, Jinjing Zhao, Yitong Wang, Yunuo Chen, Baining Guo, Zheng-Jun Zha
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18097v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18097v1)

Post-training is crucial for improving text-to-image models, yet existing pipelines typically rely on manually curated training data and fixed training distributions. We introduce APT (Agentic Post-Training), a fully automated framework that adaptively post-trains a base model according to its evolving capability profile. APT coordinates three agents to construct evaluation sets from user requirements, diagnose model-specific strengths and failure modes, and generate reinforcement learning data with an adaptive prompt distribution that emphasizes underperforming capabilities. By repeatedly evaluating, diagnosing, and updating the generator, APT lets the current model's behavior shape its next training tasks, forming a feedback loop inspired by recursive self-improvement. For general-purpose settings without user-specified evaluation scenarios, we further develop an evaluation harness that enables broad and diverse evaluation set generation. Experiments across multiple base models, benchmarks, and generation architectures demonstrate that APT is model-agnostic, benchmark-agnostic, architecture-agnostic, and fully automated.

### [Process Rewards for Outcome-Guided Reasoning Steps in LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02341v2)

- **Authors:** Mohammad Rezaei, Jens Lehmann, Sahar Vahdati
- **Published:** 2026-02-08
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv.org
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02341v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02341v2)

Mathematical reasoning in large language models has improved substantially with reinforcement learning using verifiable rewards, where final answers can be checked automatically and converted into reliable training signals. Most such pipelines optimize outcome correctness only, which yields sparse feedback for long, multi-step solutions and offers limited guidance on intermediate reasoning errors. Recent work therefore introduces process reward models (PRMs) to score intermediate steps and provide denser supervision. In practice, PRM scores are often imperfectly aligned with final correctness and can reward locally fluent reasoning that still ends in an incorrect answer. When optimized as absolute rewards, such signals can amplify fluent failure modes and induce reward hacking. We propose PROGRS, a framework that leverages PRMs while keeping outcome correctness dominant. PROGRS treats process rewards as relative preferences within outcome groups rather than absolute targets. We introduce outcome-conditioned centering, which shifts PRM scores of incorrect trajectories to have zero mean within each prompt group. It removes systematic bias while preserving informative rankings. PROGRS combines a frozen quantile-regression PRM with a multi-scale coherence evaluator. We integrate the resulting centered process bonus into Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) without auxiliary objectives or additional trainable components. Across MATH-500, AMC, AIME, MinervaMath, and OlympiadBench, PROGRS consistently improves Pass@1 over outcome-only baselines and achieves stronger performance with fewer rollouts. These results show that outcome-conditioned centering enables safe and effective use of process rewards for mathematical reasoning.

### [Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure? Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614v1)

- **Authors:** Ashutosh Hathidara
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18614v1)

Controlling an acting LLM agent means deciding, at each step of a long-horizon task, whether to trust its next action, abstain, or hand off to a human. That decision needs a per-step confidence signal predicting whether the current rollout will eventually succeed. The agent's own self-reported verbalized confidence is the natural candidate, but existing reinforcement learning methods calibrate it against the correctness of a decision at the current step. We propose a method to calibrate it against the eventual outcome of a multi-step episode: under GRPO, the group of rollouts for a task supplies success labels, and a proper scoring rule scores each step's confidence against the realized outcome. In offline RL on recorded trajectories of a Qwen2.5-7B agent in ALFWorld, TextWorld, and WebShop environments, the AUROC of step confidence against episode outcome rises from near chance to 0.68-0.85, and the top-10% of steps the agent is most confident about succeed ~4.3x more often than average. In online RL on the live ALFWorld agent, four calibration rewards improve early-step AUROC, expected calibration error goes from 0.42 to below 0.12, and selective execution improves over both the untrained agent and a format-only RL control, without degrading task success. We also introduce an evaluation protocol that separates episode depth, task difficulty, and attempt-level foresight, which shows where the gain lies: the trained agent recognizes from its first step which tasks it will fail, while telling a succeeding attempt from a failing one at the same task is harder, with some early-window signal emerging on TextWorld.

### [TSRL: Trigger-based Supervised Reinforcement Learning for Continual Agent Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17726v1)

- **Authors:** Yuqian Fu, Kaiwen Jiang, Zijun Song, Jiawei Wang, Yuanheng Zhu, Dongbin Zhao
- **Published:** 2026-10-13
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17726v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17726v1)

Large language model (LLM) agents excel at complex, multi-step tasks but suffer from catastrophic forgetting during continual learning--acquiring new skills often degrades previously mastered capabilities. Existing post-training paradigms face a fundamental trade-off: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is sample-efficient but prone to severe forgetting, while Reinforcement Learning (RL) preserves prior capabilities but converges slowly without expert guidance. To bridge this gap, we introduce Trigger-based Supervised Reinforcement Learning (TSRL), a framework that integrates the strengths of both paradigms through an adaptive, state-dependent expert supervision mechanism. TSRL employs RL as the optimization backbone, activating SFT selectively on "hard states" where the policy fails. Theoretically, we show that restricting supervision to failure states derives an upper bound on the cumulative parameter updates, thereby reducing forgetting compared to full SFT. Empirically, TSRL achieves consistent gains across three agent benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop, and Deep Search) at both 7B and 32B scales. In continual learning scenarios, our method retains up to 9.6 points more capability on prior tasks than SFT->RL baselines, while matching or exceeding target-task performance.
