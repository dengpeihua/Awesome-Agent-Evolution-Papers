### [Where is Physical RSI capability from? An Observational Scaling Law of LLM-as-policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26848v1)

- **Authors:** Zekai Li, Jiayi Yu, Jinlu Zhang, Luyang Niu, Zekun Qi, Jun Zheng, Xuchuan Chen, Dairu Liu, Yintianrun Zhang, He Wang, Li Yi
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26848v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26848v1)

In this paper, we systematically investigate the mechanisms, scaling behavior, and generalization of physical Recursive self-improvement. We introduce PhysRSI-eval to quantify physical RSI through a five-axis metric: First-Attempt Performance, Efficiency, Success, Budget and Improvement from failure. Different VLMs are evaluated on 18 manipulation tasks under a fixed interaction budget. The analysis of experimental results motivates the Physical Barrel Hypothesis: different capabilities may constrain different parts of improvement. The five-axis metrics also distinguish systems with close public benchmark averages and show that high scores need not come with early or efficient gains. We study observational scaling from two perspectives: predicting the relative performance of VLM agents from their public capabilities, and forecasting later progress from early physical feedback. Together, these analyses ask how quickly and how far an agent can improve. Moreover, we conduct workspace interventions and then analyze what improvement leaves behind. Removing history reduces late-round success, frozen workspaces remain useful on unseen layouts, and three cross-task cases achieve success earlier. The results suggest that scene-specific adaptation and reusable experience can coexist.

### [HarnessPro: Recursive Harness Self-Improvement with Pareto Optimization for Performance-Cost Trade-offs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26770v1)

- **Authors:** Zhuoshi Pan, ZhiYuan Feng, Yukuo Cen, Honglin Lin, Junru Lu, Zhongxia Chen, Shuo Yang, H. Vicky Zhao, Jie Tang
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26770v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26770v1)

Harness development plays a crucial role in building capable AI agents. Recent approaches improve harnesses through recursive self-improvement (RSI), but their performance gains often come with higher execution costs. We introduce HarnessPro, a label-free method for evolving harnesses to improve task performance while reducing execution cost. In this setting, candidate harnesses are evaluated using LLM-generated tests without access to ground-truth solutions or official tests. Specialized agents diagnose execution trajectories, plan updates with different performance-cost priorities, and implement them as candidate harnesses. Rather than returning a single harness, HarnessPro uses normalized weighted squared-distance selection to provide cost-focused (lite), balanced (medium), and performance-focused (heavy) harnesses for different deployment budgets. Selected harnesses enter the next iteration, where execution feedback guides further updates. Our experiments cover Terminal-Bench 2, SWE-Bench Pro, and ProgramBench, using GPT-5.5 with Codex and GLM-5.2 with Claude Code. Across these benchmarks using GPT-5.5 with Codex, the heavy mode of HarnessPro achieves an average pass-rate gain of 2.0 percentage points and an average per-task cost reduction of 26.1% relative to RHO. This advantage also holds when using GLM-5.2 with Claude Code. Code is available at https://github.com/pzs19/HarnessPro

### [BetaMem: Learning to Remember a Changing World for Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26554v1)

- **Authors:** Mai Liu, He Sun, Siru Zhong, Kerun Chen, Hao Yan, Xiaohui Lv, Shuai Tao, Wulong Liu
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26554v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26554v1)

Long-horizon embodied agents face temporal confusion and incomplete or distorted records, recurring errors from fixed memory rules, and growing storage and retrieval costs. We present BetaMem, a framework for building and evolving traceable long-term memory through six linked layers: working, spatial, entity, episodic, experiential, and procedural memory. A dual-level write contract constrains operation executability and source support for content: supervised fine-tuning learns compliant memory operations, while reinforcement learning combines repeated sampling with teacher verification of the original observations to reward complete, accurate records and penalize omissions, distortion, and unsupported content. We formulate failure-driven policy recursive self-improvement through diagnosis along the causal sequence of memory operations and local rule repairs gated by independent replay. Atomic tools frame storage, updates, retrieval, and forgetting as cost-utility allocation under storage and read budgets. Using the open-source Qwen3.5-9B model for memory generation, BetaMem achieves overall scores of 48.08% on M3-Bench-robot, 92.08% on LoCoMo, 72.6% on OpenEQA EM-EQA, and 62.4% on EgoLifeQA, the highest among the listed memory methods under their respective evaluation protocols. Training the model for memory generation raises the contract pass rate from 50.9% to 86.2% while reducing output tokens per stored action by 47.3%. A separate Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct evaluation on EMem-Bench achieves an average success rate of 38.4% and an average error recurrence rate of 24.9%. We perform best among evaluated methods using comparably sized open-source models. Category results show that we have advantages in integrating information across observations, recalling events over long histories, and inferring personal habits.

### [VibeTraining-Bench: Data-Trainer-Harness Co-Design for Iterative System Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26342v1)

- **Authors:** Ruotian Peng, Yuanyi Ren, Shuaiqi Liu, Xin Guo, Fang Zhu, Yangyi Huang, Yue Wu, Zheng Wang, Yandong Wen, Zhihang Fu
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26342v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26342v1)

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) requires agents to diagnose system bottlenecks and coordinate improvements across training and inference. We refer to the agents' capability to jointly optimize training data, the trainer, and the inference harness as cross-component co-design. We introduce VibeTraining-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating this capability across tasks spanning terminal operation, software engineering, multi-turn tool use, and mathematical reasoning. Within each task, research agents start from an identical initial agent system under a fixed resource budget. Guided by validation feedback, they iteratively refine data, trainers, and inference harnesses, then select and submit a final agent system for evaluation on the test set. To prevent reward hacking, we enforce three safeguards: test data isolation, separate research and grading containers, and network restrictions for research agents. Our evaluation results show that frontier research agents substantially improve target agent systems. For example, on SWE-bench Verified, GPT-6-Astra raises the target agent system's performance from 32.9% to 53.7%, comparable to the human-developed instruct model's 55.4%. However, there is still room to improve their ability to co-design and iteratively refine data, trainer, and harness to achieve cumulative performance gains. We hope that VibeTraining-Bench will serve as a unified platform for advancing RSI, deepening our understanding of cross-component optimization for agent systems. Code and data are available at https://github.com/D2I-ai/VibeTrainingBench.

### [Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858v1)

- **Authors:** Xiaoyin Chen, Yizheng Huang, Yinglun Xu, Ziyun Zhao, Emmett McQuinn, Julie Liu, Hope Yao
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25858v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25858v1)

Large language model agents are often deployed in environments where new knowledge, tools, and workflows keep arriving. Such an agent is therefore expected to adapt continually, learning new tasks as they arrive without forgetting earlier ones. This resembles the continual learning problem, but at a pace that conventional gradient updates cannot sustain. Nonparametric self-evolving agents are a natural alternative: they adapt by rewriting their own harness, such as prompts, memory, and skills, from task feedback while the model weights stay frozen. We study whether such agents are effective continual learners across four drifting task streams that shift in topic, workflow, or domain (built from BIRD, BFCL, and ScienceWorld). Evaluating three representative methods spanning experience retrieval and self-modification (ReasoningBank, GEPA, and Meta-Harness), we find that although self-evolution improves aggregate performance on most streams, higher plasticity consistently comes at the cost of stability. Specifically, self-modifying methods apply global edits to a shared prompt or workspace, interfering with unseen tasks and overwriting earlier knowledge as the stream shifts. Conversely, similarity-based memory retrieval localizes updates across distant domains, but still retrieves foreign memories on unseen tasks and suffers from retrieval collision when similar queries require conflicting workflows. These findings show that current self-evolving agents remain incomplete continual learners.

### [How Effectively Do Security Skills Guide Coding Agents in Vulnerability Detection in Linux Kernel?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25705v1)

- **Authors:** Haoran Xue, Nikta Akbarpour, Gias Uddin, Song Wang
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CR, cs.SE
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25705v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25705v1)

Large language model (LLM) coding agents, such as Claude Code, are increasingly used for security auditing, with task-specific skills providing reusable vulnerability-analysis procedures. However, their effectiveness in detecting real vulnerabilities and the reasons for their failures remain unclear. We conduct the first large-scale empirical study of 37 security skills using Claude Code with four underlying LLMs on 100 real Linux kernel vulnerabilities across ten classes, totaling 14800 runs. We evaluate vulnerability detection at the function, statement, and vulnerability-class levels. Results show that 24.4 percent of runs locate the correct function, 18.3 percent identify both the function and responsible statement, and 15.7 percent correctly classify the vulnerability. Our failure analysis reveals limitations in evidence acquisition, state-relation reasoning, code interpretation, candidate verification, investigation coverage, and reporting. Based on these findings, we propose SKILL-SIF, a self-improving framework that iteratively evolves security skills by learning from missed vulnerabilities and failure patterns. We evolve five top-performing skills using the original benchmark and evaluate them on 100 previously unseen vulnerabilities. SKILL-SIF improves fully correct vulnerability detection by up to eight times (from 2 percent to 16 percent) on unseen vulnerabilities, while aggregate accuracy across four LLMs increases by 1.85 times (from 6.05 percent to 11.20 percent). These findings highlight the limitations of existing security skills and demonstrate the potential of failure-driven skill evolution to improve vulnerability detection in coding agents.

### [Compile Once, Run Cheap: Frozen, Portable Skills for Embodied Agents, and What Recursive Self-Improvement Adds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25114v1)

- **Authors:** Jingwen Bai, Guochao Bai, Canbo Zhang, Shuangqi Li, Minchao Hu, Weichun Huang, Shuang Liu
- **Published:** 2026-10-18
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25114v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25114v1)

Embodied AI lacks the data-rich pretraining that gave language models their agents and skills, so new manipulation capability costs demonstrations per task or a frontier model in every episode. We study a third route: a strong general model acts only as an offline author that writes, runs, diagnoses and repairs a manipulation skill in simulation until it passes an exam on layouts drawn after freezing; the frozen skill then runs as code alone or under a cheap caller that reads only its description. We call this skill-level recursive self-improvement Skill-RSI and specify a compile protocol (isolated, audited author; hidden validation layouts; no-feedback and inherit-only controls; tamper-evident ledger) and a reuse protocol that runs the identical files with executors from another model family. On 7 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks with cluttered tables, Claude Code compiled 4 tasks into 7 frozen skills, 5 from a first draft needing only qualification, at USD 4.29-8.22 of author cost each (USD 55 per skill counting the 13 failed campaigns). The other three tasks did not compile in 18 campaigns, including five with GPT-6 and a pre-registered 150-layout run: on the hardest task every campaign scored 78-95 of 100 but had 3-16 safety aborts, failing the gate of 95% success with zero aborts; a 100-episode exam bounds an abort rate only to 3.6%. On hidden layouts, repair that inherits the previous version beat from-scratch rewriting in 8 of 8 controlled campaigns (sign test p = 0.004); an inherit-only lineage attributes most of the gap to inheritance, with development feedback adding a little (3 of 3, p = 0.125). Write-once campaigns found no detectable first-draft effect of practice (minimum detectable effect 14 points). The frozen skills kept 148/150 successes on new layouts with no model and 147/150 with a DeepSeek-flash caller at USD 0.00028-0.00054 per episode; no direct-control baseline was run.

### [CoEvo-RSI: When Harnesses Evolve, Which Models Should Follow?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25078v1)

- **Authors:** Jiaqi Su, Guibin Zhang, Jie-Jing Shao, Cong Pang, Tiankuo Yao, Ivor Tsang, Haiyan Yin, Shuicheng Yan
- **Published:** 2026-10-18
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25078v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25078v1)

Model--harness co-evolution has emerged as a prominent paradigm for recursive self-improving (RSI) agents, jointly evolving their execution scaffolds and intrinsic capabilities. We study the persistent yet often overlooked brain--body mismatch problem in this paradigm, where an evolving harness (body) demands behaviors its model (brain) has not learned or restricts capabilities it already possesses. We introduce CoEvo-RSI, a RSI framework that advances model--harness co-evolution through agent-driven, bidirectional historical inheritance. Drawing on a shared evolutionary archive and execution feedback, a meta-agent coordinates harness development from historical designs with model checkpoint selection or merging and subsequent adaptation under the selected harness, aligning inherited capabilities with evolving execution requirements to reduce brain--body mismatch. Using only supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on its own rejection-sampled trajectories, CoEvo-RSI makes Qwen3.5-9B competitive with larger models across diverse vertical applications, including multi-agent failure attribution, role-aware text-to-SQL, and smart-home instruction following.
