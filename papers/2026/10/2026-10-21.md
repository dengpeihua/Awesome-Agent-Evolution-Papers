## Recursive Self-Improvement

### [MELD: Recursive Self-Improvement via Hierarchical Coordination for Multi-Agent Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24454v1)

- **Authors:** Sizhe Tang, Zuyuan Zhang, Zeyu Fang, Tian Lan
- **Published:** 2026-10-17
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24454v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24454v1)

Multi-agent scientific discovery with large language models raises a coupled learning problem: research assignments determine both which experiments are pursued and the experiences from which agents adapt. We introduce MELD, a unified hierarchical test-time training framework that jointly adapts a team-level Coordinator policy and agent-level individual Scientist policies within a single discovery process. The Coordinator generates a joint portfolio of search branches and research directives over a shared derivation tree, while Scientists develop hypotheses and execution plans implemented by a shared frozen Executor. Both levels receive alternating GRPO-style parameter updates under a shared smooth best-of-budget objective, allowing team organization and individual research behavior to co-evolve in a recursive self-improvement loop. Our analysis establishes objective alignment for the hierarchy, characterizes conditions for coordination gains over independent parallel discovery and the first-order benefit of private adaptation, and derives a finite-budget regret bound for adapted models. On 22 MLGym and MLE-bench tasks, MELD is best on 20 of them and achieves the highest aggregate normalized scores on MLGym, using a 4B backbone for coordination and research. Most baselines use proprietary Scientists, and all systems share one frozen Executor.

### [Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344v2)

- **Authors:** Minhua Lin, Zijun Wang, Zewen Liu, Zhaotian Weng, Bing He, Zhan Shi, Benoit Dumoulin, Xiang Zhang, Suhang Wang, Hanqing Lu
- **Published:** 2026-10-16
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23344v2)

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate AI research, including language-model (LM) development, offering a potential path toward recursive self-improvement. However, turning experimental feedback into a learned ability to propose effective improvements across models faces two challenges. First, training such an ability with real feedback is expensive: reinforcement learning (RL) requires evaluating tens of thousands of proposals, each potentially requiring a full model-training run. Even at ten GPU-hours per run, reward collection alone would consume hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours, motivating a cheaper surrogate. Second, surrogate feedback can become unreliable as policy optimization favors overestimated proposals when the proposal distribution shifts. To address these challenges, we propose LM2LM, which trains LLMs with RL to generate effective LM improvement proposals. To provide scalable feedback, we use a research world model (RWM), a frozen LLM that predicts proposal gains from real experimental records provided in context, as the reward model for RL. To maintain feedback reliability, we periodically execute current-policy proposals ranked highly by the RWM and add their measured outcomes to its context. Across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments, real-execution evaluations on held-out tasks show that LM2LM enables Qwen3.8-27B to outperform GPT-6-sol, even when GPT-6-sol is given 300 real experimental records in context, while requiring 33% lower data-generation cost. LM2LM also generalizes to a model family unseen during training.

### [Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791v1)

- **Authors:** Zhepei Wei, Wenlin Yao, Baolin Peng, Danqing Wang, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng, Yu Meng, Jianfeng Gao
- **Published:** 2026-10-16
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22791v1)

Large language model (LLM) agents have shown promising potential in coding tasks, and their performance is jointly determined by the policy model and the harness that mediates their interaction with the environment. Indeed, harness and policy are mutually reinforcing and evolving only one component is suboptimal---a harness tuned for one checkpoint may not target the failure modes of an updated policy, while policy learning remains limited to the trajectories elicited by a fixed harness. In light of this, we propose Self-HPO, a recursive self-improving framework for coding agents that alternates harness and policy optimization, using the evolving policy as its own harness proposer. Specifically, in each round, the current policy first analyzes its own failures and proposes new harness candidates (H-step). The policy is then updated through reinforcement learning (RL) under the selected harness (P-step), and hard tasks identified during training form the next harness search set, keeping harness optimization targeted to the policy's evolving failure modes. Across three coding benchmarks, Self-HPO consistently outperforms harness-only and policy-only baselines, improving Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate from 57.8% to 64.8%, with gains generalizing to out-of-distribution settings. Further analysis shows that both optimized components retain gains even when paired with their base counterparts, while their joint gain exceeds the sum of their individual gains. Self-HPO also improves reliability (Pass@1) and expands the capability frontier (Pass@k) over the base agent. Additionally, we show that with a stronger external model (e.g., Opus 4.8) as the harness proposer, the same framework yields a further performance boost.

## Reinforcement Learning

### [Mitigating Reward Hacking in Preference Reward Models with Evolving Penalties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26496v2)

- **Authors:** Afonso Baldo, António Farinhas, Marcos Treviso, Ricardo Rei, Nuno M. Guerreiro
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26496v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26496v2)

Reinforcement learning for language models has moved from preference reward models to verifiable rewards, which sustain much longer optimization. Preference models are prone to reward hacking: the policy raises their score without improving response quality, or even while degrading it. Yet for open-ended tasks, where quality can neither be verified nor fully written down as rubrics, they remain valuable. We introduce Dynalize, which mitigates reward hacking online, during policy training. Periodically and asynchronously, a language model analyzes training rollouts and writes penalties for undesirable behaviors that the reward model appears to reward; a judge applies them to every rollout. In clinical counseling and creative writing, training on the reward model alone raises its score continuously but ultimately leads to a decline in external benchmarks; Dynalize sustains longer training and results in improved downstream performance. We find that our method suppresses patterns incentivized by preference models that, without penalties, spread to most responses, and even to the model's reasoning, which the reward model does not explicitly score. Furthermore, we show that its gains hold in multi-turn training, and even after we retrain the reward model on the discovered behaviors.

### [SPAR: Structural Pruning and Action Recovery for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26487v1)

- **Authors:** Shenli Zheng, Zhou Fang, Yuan Du, Yijiang Liu, Li Du
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26487v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26487v1)

The limited onboard computing resources of robots make the inference demands of vision-language-action (VLA) models costly to accommodate. Reducing these demands through aggressive compression, however, can degrade manipulation performance beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone recovers. We present SPAR (Structural Pruning and Action Recovery), which simultaneously prunes the hidden and feed-forward intermediate dimensions of the large language model (LLM) backbone through pruning-aware tuning, then applies reinforcement learning (RL) to recover policy performance. Applied to $π_{0.5}$, SPAR reduces the language backbone from 2.34B to 0.09B parameters and the full model from 3.37B to 1.12B, removing approximately 96% and 67% of their parameters, respectively. At this compression level, RL raises average success across the four LIBERO suites from 84.55% to 93.45%, an 8.90-percentage-point gain. The compressed policy achieves a $1.98\times$ full-policy inference speedup over the unpruned model. These results identify substantial intermediate-dimension redundancy and show that post-pruning RL can recover manipulation performance in compact policies.

### [From Cause to Effect: Generating Intermediate Event Chains in Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26275v1)

- **Authors:** Ridouane Ghermi, Vicky Kalogeiton, Ivan Laptev
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** 🏅 Selected top venue: EMNLP 2026<br>📣 Acceptance first observed: 2026-10-21 (actual acceptance date unverified)
- **Venue:** EMNLP 2026
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26275v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26275v1)

Video-Language Models (VLMs) excel at visual recognition but struggle with causal reasoning. Current evaluations are flawed: multiple-choice QA allows shortcuts, and open-ended QA lacks robust automatic evaluation. We introduce Causal Chain Generation (CCG), a novel task where models have to generate the sequence of intermediate events connecting a cause and an effect. This structured setup demands genuine reasoning and enables reliable automatic scoring. We accompany this task with two complementary metrics: SODA-Embedding, which measures the semantic matching of a generated chain against a reference, and Causal-QA, which probes the chain's causal coherence by asking whether it contains the specific causal facts needed to reconstruct the narrative. We provide two test sets: SF20K-CC, a manually annotated diagnostic on 20 short films, and CMD-CC, a movie clip benchmark derived from audio descriptions. Our analysis reveals that state-of-the-art VLMs struggle with narrative causality understanding, frequently hallucinating or omitting key events. To address this, we fine-tune VLMs with GRPO using a composite reward that combines a format reward, SODA-Embedding, and Causal-QA accuracy, demonstrating substantial improvements over strong baselines.

### [Honeyed Words, Hidden Daggers: Benchmarking and Training LLMs to See Through Mock Politeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26059v1)

- **Authors:** Caixin Kang, Sitong Gong, Tianyu Yan, Kaipeng Zhang, Yoichi Sato, Yifei Huang
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26059v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26059v1)

The Chinese idiom "kou mi fu jian" ("honeyed words, hidden daggers") names speech that is polite in form but hostile in function. We present ZhenhuanBench, a 1,592-item benchmark built from expert commentary on Empresses in the Palace, covering speaker intent, emotion-language mismatch, and per-listener uptake. Text-only evaluation covers 34 LLMs. GPT-6 Astra leads the leaderboard at 73.48% overall, while its listener-reaction score is 58.37%. The annotations also supply supervised traces and string-verifiable training rewards. With 338 gold traces, an 8B model outperforms teacher distillation on identical items. Subsequent RL saturates, and chance correction reduces the estimated recoverable performance gap by a factor of 7.5. Our CPR-GRPO loop (Compress-Probe-Replenish) uses this estimate to weight gold supervision, reaching 67.0 on public multiple-choice tasks and 74.5 under the training-matched hidden protocol. Without target-task training, CPR outperforms Base, SFT, and GRPO across six Chinese and English pragmatic and sarcasm evaluations. On three external datasets, training with CPR yields higher mean Macro-F1 than SFT and GRPO.

### [FrameGRPO: Incentivizing Video Reasoning via Multi-Frame Rollouts and Temporal Credit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26022v1)

- **Authors:** Jiaxuan Sun, Biao Wang, Shuai Li
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26022v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26022v1)

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has improved video reasoning in multimodal large language models. However, standard video GRPO varies only linguistic trajectories while conditioning every rollout in a group on the same sampled frames, which can produce homogeneous rewards and weaken group-relative learning when the shared visual evidence is insufficient or distracting. We propose \textbf{FrameGRPO}, a simple and effective reinforcement-learning framework that requires neither auxiliary networks nor changes to the model architecture. Specifically, FrameGRPO combines two lightweight components: (1) \textbf{Multi-Frame Rollouts}, which use only uniform temporal subsampling of a single maximal-frame decode to construct aligned views that diversify visual evidence and reasoning trajectories; and (2) \textbf{Gated Temporal Efficiency Credit}, which selectively reinforces a correct lower-ratio trajectory when all rollouts in a fixed full-ratio reference subset fail, without imposing an unconditional preference for fewer frames. Extensive experiments across diverse video understanding and reasoning benchmarks demonstrate robust overall improvements over standard GRPO, highlighting temporal-view diversification as an effective mechanism for exploration and credit assignment in video MLLMs.

### [Speech LLMs That Breathe: Modeling Non-Verbal Vocalizations in Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25942v1)

- **Authors:** Heyang Liu, Lixin Zhang, Jiayi Huang, Ziyang Cheng, Yuhao Wang, Ronghua Wu, Qunshan Gu, Yanfeng Wang, Yu Wang
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25942v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25942v1)

The user experience of speech interaction models has improved, benefiting from the modeling of paralinguistic attributes and advances in speech reconstruction paradigms. However, human communication follows extensive norms regarding non-verbal vocalizations (NVVs) spanning respiratory, affective, and discourse. The omission of these events imposes inherent limitations on the naturalness of current approaches. In this work, we cover 13 major types of NVVs and construct a natural dialogue dataset that conforms to human conversational norms through an automated synthesis pipeline. We enhance the ability of speech interaction models to generate contextually appropriate NVV events through multi-stage fine-tuning and NVV-aware GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization). Based on distinct modeling paradigms, we substantially improve the expressiveness of VocalNet and VITA-Qinyu in empathy interactions and role-play dialogues, and further discuss the architectural necessity for achieving high-quality natural speech generation.

### [Anatomy of Off-Policy Policy Gradient: Importance Sampling, KL Regularization, and Baselines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25725v1)

- **Authors:** Haoqun Cao, Yurun Yuan, Tengyang Xie
- **Published:** 2026-10-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI

Reinforcement learning has become a widely used approach to post-training large language models. In a typical implementation, learning is off-policy: The policy generating the responses differs from the policy being trained because of training-inference mismatch or policy staleness in asynchronous training. Under regularized importance sampling, the importance-weighted policy gradient is no longer invariant to constant shifts in the reward. Whether both forms of centering should be used simultaneously depends on whether importance sampling is applied at the sequence level or at the token level.
