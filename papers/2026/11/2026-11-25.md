### [Self-Upgrading Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31117v1)

- **Authors:** Jiajun Fan, Neil He, Qinglong Zheng, Changyi Yang, Tong Wei, Andy Mao, Zetian Li, Chaoran Cheng, Hui Ren, Ruihan Guo, Xianrui Zhong, Xizheng Yu, Jingyuan Li, Xi Zhu, Kaiqu Liang, Chang Xu, Zun Qian, Zidi Xiong, Yuezhu Zhao, Xu Huang, Ge Liu
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31117v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31117v1)

An embodied agent built on a language model goes to work with the weights it was given and keeps them for life. To get better, it needs more training, or reflection, memory, search or prompts that cost every step and are lost when the task changes. Editing weights by reasoning, weight-space programming, could end this, but has stayed with models that only reason or relied on a far larger commercial model. We introduce the self-upgrading embodied agent, to our knowledge the first that learns from its own experience by upgrading its own weights on the job, between its tasks, with no training, no gradient, no reward model, no second model, no test-time scaling and no harness or prompt engineering. By day a 27B agent works in a household environment. At night it reads its record, finds many of its steps wasted on replies that reach the token limit before any action, traces the habit to one direction in its hidden states, and rewrites one tensor of its weights. An agent that rewrites itself must first never break itself, so it is its own examiner: it tests every change against its unchanged self on practice tasks and keeps only what holds. When an edit holds, the next day it is a better agent, at the same cost per step: over thousands of held-out episodes, 48 fresh seeds a task, its one kept edit makes it complete significantly more tasks, in fewer steps, with fewer wasted and fewer tokens. This self-improvement is multi-round, stable and continual: through the nights that follow, the upgrade holds, without collapse. Of four sizes of its family, only the 27B agent does this. From here on, an embodied agent need not keep training and inference apart: as it works, it gathers its own experience and upgrades itself, with no separate pre-training or post-training and no human intervention. We believe this opens lifelong continual learning for robots: stable, continual, recursive self-improvement.

### [Real2Worlds2Real: Scaling Self-Improving Agentic Real2Sim2Real for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31098v1)

- **Authors:** Jinzhong Gu, Jiakai Chen, Xinyi Cao, Yan Shen, Hangke Sui, Shutian Zheng, Xiang Xiao, Haoran Lu, Fan Xu, Lu Shi, Daochen Shi, Ruihua Zhang, Wei Zhan, Masayoshi Tomizuka, Ruihai Wu
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31098v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31098v1)

Scaling robot manipulation learning remains challenging due to the high cost of collecting real-world interaction data. Real-to-Sim-to-Real (Real2Sim2Real) offers a promising route by enabling policy learning in simulation grounded in real-world observations, yet scaling this paradigm across diverse tasks and physical interactions remains difficult. We introduce Real2Worlds2Real, a self-improving agentic framework that integrates scalable simulation-world construction with policy development. Given limited visual observations and task descriptions, its tool-orchestration harness constructs faithful digital twins and expands them into diverse, physically executable digital cousins. The agent adaptively selects tools and revisits construction decisions, using visual and execution feedback to refine geometry and physical behavior. Building on these worlds, the agent orchestrates policy development by coordinating data generation, policy learning, and refinement based on task requirements and simulation rollout feedback. Across both stages, the agent distills validated procedures into reusable skills that guide subsequent tasks. Experiments demonstrate improved world fidelity and successful real-world policy transfer, with performance gains from increasing world diversity and interaction-data volume. Skill reuse further improves world-building efficiency and long-horizon task success.

### [Beyond Self-Consistency: Recursive Self-Improvement at Test Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965v1)

- **Authors:** Jianyu Wu, Yizhou Wang, Encheng Su, Jinouwen Zhang, Pengze Li, Lintao Wang, Chen Tang, Aoran Wang, Wanli Ouyang, Shixiang Tang
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30965v1)

Large language models (LLMs) that continue improving after deployment (e.g., recursive self-improvement) offer a promising path toward general intelligence. Current test-time methods pursue this goal on unlabeled questions, primarily relying on self-consistency: they generate independent solutions and use majority voting to select an answer or construct training targets. This leaves earlier reasoning unused at inference and can reinforce an incorrect majority during training. Humans instead build on earlier attempts to preserve useful reasoning, correct mistakes and improve their problem-solving strategies. Following this principle, we propose Test-Time Recursive Self-Improvement (TT-RSI), a label-free framework for improving LLMs at test time through two mutually reinforcing components: (1) In-Context RSI repeatedly re-solves each question using the full previous solution as a reference, letting the model decide whether and how to use it so that inference compute builds on earlier reasoning. (2) In-Weight RSI distills successive refinements into the model's weights, enabling progress beyond an initially wrong majority and theoretically bringing the first attempt toward the model's infinite-step refinement limit. The updated model answers better and refines more effectively, generating stronger references for subsequent training. Extensive experiments show that TT-RSI improves average accuracy by +13.89% over the base model and outperforms the strongest self-consistency-based method by +4.79% under the same test-time scaling budget.

### [Self-Evolving Evaluation Environments for Computer-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30962v1)

- **Authors:** Wenhao Wang, Panrong Tong, Quyu Kong, Pengxiang Li, Haowen Yang, chenglin Cai, Siheng Chen, Yue Wang
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30962v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30962v1)

Computer-use agents (CUAs) are rapidly improving, yet their evaluation remains largely static. Existing benchmarks provide a strong foundation, but fixed difficulty ceilings, limited feature coverage, and coarse aggregate scores progressively weaken their discriminative and diagnostic value. We introduce CUA-SEE, a closed-loop evaluation environment that co-evolves tasks and executable verifiers from rollout evidence. It disentangles three objectives: benchmark evolution expands evaluation scope through cohort-calibrated difficulty and coverage growth; diagnostic evolution increases resolution through agent-conditional capability-boundary probes; and verifier evolution improves precision by translating changing requirements into state-grounded checkpoints with adversarial validation. Across multiple agents, applications, and rounds, CUA-SEE generates challenging, measurable tasks while preserving process-level evidence. Our results position adaptive evaluation as a scalable counterpart to self-evolving agents, continuously tracking what agents can do, where their boundaries lie, and how reliably progress is measured.

### [DriveDreamer-RSI: Recursive Self-Improvement of Driving Perception and Agentic Data Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30671v1)

- **Authors:** Dingyi Yao, Tingdong Yu, Guosheng Zhao, Chaojun Ni, Xiaofeng Wang, Jiming Mao, Guan Huang, Lihui Peng, Zheng Zhu
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30671v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30671v1)

Controllable generation can expand training data for driving perception, but visual realism alone does not ensure learning gains. Effective generation must adapt both what data to generate and how to construct them as the perception model evolves. In this paper, we present DriveDreamer-RSI, a framework for recursive self-improvement of driving perception and agentic data generation through feedback between perception learning and data construction. Its outer loop implements Gap-Guided Target Adaptation, generating labeled videos from current perception gaps and updating targets after training. Its inner loop uses a vision-language agent to iteratively edit scenes through observation and execution feedback. Across rounds, Learning-Feedback-Driven Strategy Refinement derives reusable construction advice from execution experience and downstream learning outcomes. On the nuScenes validation set, DriveDreamer-RSI surpasses the dataset-replay baseline by 4.09 mAP and 4.13 NDS points using approximately half the synthetic frames.

### [AutoSECO: Automated Skill Evolution for Cross-Problem Combinatorial Optimization with General-Purpose LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30139v1)

- **Authors:** Zirui Zhu, Cong Zhang, Ni Zhang, Kuan Xu, Zhiguang Cao
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30139v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30139v1)

General-purpose large language model (LLM) agents can develop complete solvers for combinatorial optimization problems. Their specialization, however, often relies on workflows engineered for individual problems, limiting the reuse of development expertise. We propose AutoSECO, an automated skill evolution method that learns reusable solver-development guidance from experience. The challenge is that external evaluation measures the completed solver, while the skill guides decisions during development. AutoSECO connects this feedback to guidance deficiencies by tracing performance shortfalls through solver code and recorded development decisions. Revisions are expressed as general instructions and retained only if a fresh solver developed under the revised skill achieves a lower mean evaluation gap. Evolved solely on the traveling salesperson problem (TSP), the skill guides fresh agents in developing solvers for capacitated vehicle routing, job-shop scheduling, and three-dimensional bin packing without further skill evolution. On TSP, the final mean benchmark gap is 0.52\%, compared with 0.98\% for the skill-evolution baseline SkillOpt. Across all four problems, the evolved skill achieves better aggregate results than no skill guidance, the initial skill, and SkillOpt. The same frozen skill also supports solver development under alternative model--harness configurations. These findings suggest that solver-development experience can be retained as reusable expertise for general-purpose LLM agents.

### [Agent on Guard: Self-Improving Runtime Intervention for Robust Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30101v1)

- **Authors:** Gengming Cui, Mingfei Han, Jiawei Zhang, Mozheng Liao, Yunkai Guo, Minghao Jin, Tingwen Du, Salman Khan, Xiaojun Chang, Zhihui Li
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO, cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30101v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30101v1)

Robot foundation policies provide learned execution capabilities, while programming agents can reason about failures and construct recovery behaviors. Combining them effectively requires learning when intervention helps and when the policy can reliably resume. This challenge is inherently temporal: the physical world continues to evolve during reasoning, and a useful recovery may become infeasible before a decision takes effect. We introduce Agent on Guard (AoG), a hierarchical framework that improves collaboration between a frozen base policy and a programming agent through physical experience. An evolving outer planner allocates control as the physical state evolves, aligning decisions with the physical clock with attention to observation freshness, remaining recovery windows, and the time required for reasoning, recovery, and subsequent execution. Coupled inner loops jointly refine intervention timing, recovery skills, and handoff conditions. Rather than judging recovery by local correction alone, AoG uses downstream task progress, failure recurrence, final outcomes, and resource consumption to improve both recovery behaviors and scheduling. This turns temporary agent solutions into reusable collaboration capabilities that complement the frozen policy. On RoboDojo-Sim, AoG achieves a success rate of 50.57% and a task score of 57.26, exceeding the strongest evaluated baseline by 11.22 percentage points and 10.68 points, respectively. It also reduces end-to-end episode latency by 80.4% and online token consumption by 85.1% relative to the GPT-6 Astra agent.

### [Blind to What They Can Fix: A Detection Gap in Self-Improving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29807v1)

- **Authors:** Teng Cao, Yu Deng, Kristian Kersting
- **Published:** 2026-11-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.SE
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29807v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29807v1)

AI agents can write programs that carry out a task without calling a model at every step, so the task runs fast and its logic stays open to inspection. Agents improve these programs by running them, studying their failures, and revising the code, a form of recursive self-improvement (RSI). We ask whether this process also yields runtime checks that can be trusted. In five task domains, with up to seven author models, agents bring their programs to high scores. Yet when the environment changes, the checks they were told to write stay silent on half or more of the resulting failures in four domains. These failures can be detected and repaired: a monitor built from records of normal runs catches most of them in three domains, and the same agents usually repair them once called. The checks do not examine the recorded relations that reveal the failures, and the strongest agent writes checks that catch most of them once told that its checks are the only thing that calls it back. An unreported failure stays unrepaired, which limits what self-improvement is worth in autonomous operation. Evaluations of self-improvement should measure not only scores but also whether a system notices its own failures.

### [Fin-RSI: A Unified Library of Financial Skills, Checks, and Self-Improvement for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29716v1)

- **Authors:** Wanghao Ye, Shwai He, Sihan Chen, Yiting Wang, Bowei Tian, Guoheng Sun, Ziyi Wang, Ziyao Wang, Yexiao He, Zheyu Shen, Meng Liu, Yuning Zhang, Meng Feng, Yifei Dong, Yanhong Qian, Yang Wang, Siyuan Peng, Yilong Dai, Zhenle Duan, Joshua Liu, Lang Xiong, Aarosh Das, Zhaoyi Liu, Daeun Jung, Aditya Sarkar, Parastoo Pilevar, Xiaochuan Gong, Yancheng Zhu, Yingying Fan, Qiong Wang, Ang Li
- **Published:** 2026-11-22
- **Priority:** —

- **Affiliations:** n/a

As large language models (LLMs) become more capable, financial agents need tools they can readily discover and use. Yet financial software is fragmented across libraries with different interfaces and conventions, making it difficult to build reliable agent workflows. We present Fin-RSI, a unified Python library of financial skills, checks, and self-improvement designed for LLM agents. It brings together tools for market analysis, forecasting, portfolio construction, and trading research through shared interfaces across models and agent frameworks. Agents can find suitable tools from natural-language requests, reuse tested implementations, and improve generated programs through checking and feedback. Developers can extend the library with new tools without rebuilding integrations for each model. Across 20 open-weight models on six trading-position tasks, tool discovery increased the proportion of episodes producing valid programs from 9.7% to 29.2%; for models with at least 14 billion parameters, it rose from 23.6% to 64.6%. We release the library and benchmark as a shared resource for the community and plan to expand its tools and task coverage as financial agents evolve.

### [LLM-Assisted Co-Optimization of RTL and Synthesis Algorithms via Bottleneck Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29582v1)

- **Authors:** Yikang Ouyang, Yang Luo, Dongsheng Zuo, Xiaoxiao Liang, Yuzhe Ma
- **Published:** 2026-11-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AR
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29582v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29582v1)

Post-synthesis power, performance, and area (PPA) depend jointly on the register-transfer-level (RTL) code and the synthesis algorithms. However, the benefits of RTL optimization may be limited by bottlenecks in synthesis algorithms, and vice versa, leaving opportunities for better PPA unexplored by methods that optimize either level alone.
