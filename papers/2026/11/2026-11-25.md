### [Beyond Self-Consistency: Recursive Self-Improvement at Test Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965v1)

- **Authors:** Jianyu Wu, Yizhou Wang, Encheng Su, Jinouwen Zhang, Pengze Li, Lintao Wang, Chen Tang, Aoran Wang, Wanli Ouyang, Shixiang Tang
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30965v1)

Large language models (LLMs) that continue improving after deployment (e.g., recursive self-improvement) offer a promising path toward general intelligence. Current test-time methods pursue this goal on unlabeled questions, primarily relying on self-consistency: they generate independent solutions and use majority voting to select an answer or construct training targets. This leaves earlier reasoning unused at inference and can reinforce an incorrect majority during training. Humans instead build on earlier attempts to preserve useful reasoning, correct mistakes and improve their problem-solving strategies. Following this principle, we propose Test-Time Recursive Self-Improvement (TT-RSI), a label-free framework for improving LLMs at test time through two mutually reinforcing components: (1) In-Context RSI repeatedly re-solves each question using the full previous solution as a reference, letting the model decide whether and how to use it so that inference compute builds on earlier reasoning. (2) In-Weight RSI distills successive refinements into the model's weights, enabling progress beyond an initially wrong majority and theoretically bringing the first attempt toward the model's infinite-step refinement limit. The updated model answers better and refines more effectively, generating stronger references for subsequent training. Extensive experiments show that TT-RSI improves average accuracy by +13.89% over the base model and outperforms the strongest self-consistency-based method by +4.79% under the same test-time scaling budget.

### [RSI-Prime: Training Models to Train Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138v1)

- **Authors:** Dewu Zheng, Yang Ye, Hongyu Zhang, Jun Yu, Yanlin Wang
- **Published:** 2026-11-17
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23138v1)

Enabling large language models (LLMs) to autonomously conduct training and improve target models is an important foundation for recursive self-improvement (RSI). As LLMs expand their role from being trained to conducting training, their model-training capability warrants dedicated study and systematic development. To this end, we construct RSI-Prime, a dataset for improving model-training capability, comprising 350 complete experimental trajectories across seven task categories. To help LLMs learn to make high-quality experimental decisions from training practice, we perform fine-grained decision identification and quality annotation, then apply decision-level loss masking to exclude supervision on low-quality behavior. Experiments with GLM-5.3 on PostTrainBench, a benchmark for autonomous post-training, show that training on RSI-Prime yields a 31.95% relative improvement in overall performance over the original GLM-5.3, highlighting a path toward future RSI systems in which models autonomously sustain their own advancement.

### [SkillDuet: Co-Evolving Models and Skills through Response Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12109v2)

- **Authors:** Xingguang Ji, Ziqiang Dong, Yubo Zhang, Xi Leng, Ninghua Yan, Yang Yang, Guanjun Jiang
- **Published:** 2026-11-09
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12109v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12109v2)

During reinforcement learning, experience can be both internalized in LLM parameters and retained externally as reusable textual skills. Parameter updates alter the model's behavior and the interaction experience from which new skills are extracted. These skills, in turn, guide the model's subsequent interactions and parameter updates. As the model's capabilities evolve, previously beneficial skills may become redundant or detrimental. We present SkillDuet, a framework that coordinates parameter updates and skill evolution through model response feedback. Skills extracted from on-policy trajectories guide parameter updates through OPD at every step. A response-based score, Strength, ranks candidate skills by the magnitude of the shift they induce in the current model's responses, and the highest-scoring skills are periodically consolidated into a persistent global skill set (Global). The updated model and Global jointly guide subsequent interactions, generating experience for further parameter and skill updates. With Qwen3-4B, SkillDuet achieves success rates of 98.57% on ALFWorld and 92.97% on WebShop, exceeding the matched SEED-API baseline by 2.86 and 5.47 percentage points, respectively. Ablations show that response-based skill selection and Global consolidation improve task performance. Further experiments show that the benefits of retained skills depend on both their content and their compatibility with the model. SkillDuet provides a concrete mechanism for model-skill co-evolution and skill-level recursive self-improvement.

### [TinyAstral: Transferring Self-Evolving Harnesses to Tiny Generalist Manipulation Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41216v2)

Robot agents built on vision-language models depend on both the planner and its harness: the tools, feedback, memory, and recovery logic around it. Can improvements to a large teacher's harness be carried into a compact agent with lower inference compute cost? We present TinyAstral, a two-stage approach. We first freeze an open-weight teacher and improve its harness through recursive self-improvement (RSI): frontier LLM agents read the teacher's trajectories and propose harness edits, and each edit is accepted only if it passes a paired statistical test. It stays close to its teacher on all three training benchmarks, and on three benchmarks never used for RSI or training it scores close to a 552B planner running the same harness. On the measured deployment episodes, it uses about one fifth of its teacher's accelerator time. Project page: https://garfield-kh.github.io/TinyAstral/

### [Training LLM Agents to Automate LLM Post-Training with RSI-Dojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03698v2)

- **Authors:** Shuzheng Gao, Zhaojian Yu, Penghao Yin, Yuling Shi, Kai Cai, Shilin He, Michael R. Lyu
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03698v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03698v2)

Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) aims to enable AI systems to autonomously build and improve AI systems themselves, including models, harnesses, and underlying infrastructure. Within this broader agenda, a central capability is enabling agents to train and improve base models. However, existing studies on automated model training primarily focused on workflow- or harness-level optimizations, rather than internalizing the ability to conduct post-training into the model itself. Developing this capability requires executable model training environments that can produce high-quality trajectories demonstrating how to select data, train models, diagnose failures, and iteratively improve results. In this paper, we introduce RSI-Dojo, a collection of executable model training environments designed to teach LLM agents to conduct post-training. To construct RSI-Dojo, we develop RSI-Craft, an automatic pipeline that converts human research artifacts into executable model training environments. RSI-Craft automatically collects research papers from top venues, instantiates datasets and metrics into a unified interface, and reproduces evaluation dependencies. It then filters tasks by data size, execution reliability, and discriminability, yielding RSI-Dojo with 281 seed model training tasks. Building on RSI-Dojo, we employ a trajectory collection method with an improved harness to collect high-quality model-training trajectories from frontier models, then retain examples through rubric-based and outcome-based filtering. We fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on these trajectories. The resulting RSI-Dojo-9B and RSI-Dojo-35B-A3B improve over their base models by 8.74 and 16.87 PostTrainBench points, respectively. RSI-Dojo-35B-A3B reaches 29.95 on PostTrainBench and 53.29 on PostTrainBench-Lite, matching the performance of frontier models such as GLM-5.2 and GPT 5.5.

### [Try Before You Evolve: Ranking-Scored Trials for Recursive Self-Improvement of Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39352v1)

- **Authors:** Xu Huang, Jiawei Feng, Jiancan Wu
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.IR
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39352v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39352v1)

Recommenders on Web platforms such as e-commerce and music streaming need their training recipes re-tuned for each dataset. Large language model (LLM) agents can now rewrite these recipes, enabling recursive self-improvement (RSI) of recommender systems. Existing agents propose one edit per round, fully retrain the recommender, and keep the edit only if its ranking quality improves. Unlike programs or patches that can be scored by execution, a recommender edit changes how the model is trained, which raises two challenges. First, ranking feedback is delayed: an edit's ranking quality is known only after full retraining, so much of the retraining budget goes to edits that are later discarded. Second, the signals available before retraining are unreliable: sampled training losses can look solved while full-ranking performance remains poor, and they are not comparable across edits that change the loss or sampler. We propose RecRSI, which generates diverse candidate edits from a diagnosis of the current model and selects one for full retraining using early ranking feedback: each candidate receives a short trial, the first steps of its full training run, scored by full-ranking NDCG@10 on held-out probe users. With two sequential recommenders on three datasets and the same number of full retrainings, RecRSI significantly outperforms existing self-evolving agents in every setting, and on both Amazon datasets reaches in its first round a ranking quality that none of them reaches in five.

### [Youtu-Parsing-Omni: One Encoder, One Schema, All Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39301v1)

- **Authors:** Yunhang Shen, Zhangquan Chen, Xiaotian Li, Zhongpeng Cai, Qing Zhou, Zekai Zhang, Kun Zhang, Zezhong Wu, Jian Hu, Haihan Gao, Heting Gao, Xin Li, Yinsong Liu, Deqian Jiang, Di Yin, Haoyu Cao, Xing Sun, Bing Liu, Yanqiu Qu, Yunfei Wu, Kun Yin, Zhixiang Wei, Yongxiang Hua, Qianyu Li, Xiaoxing Hu, Peiyuan Zhang, Lijiang Li, Dong Xu, Zhenchuan Yang, Yiliang Jiang
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39301v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39301v1)

We present Youtu-Parsing-Omni, an omni-modal parser that converts documents, images, audio, and video into structured records grounded in space and time, providing a perception layer for agents that read, locate, and cite evidence across modalities. Its design rests on one encoder for every modality and one schema for every task. Youtu-Omni-Encoder, initialized from a pre-trained language model to strengthen text reading, encodes all modalities with shared weights in a unified space-time coordinate system, and a few fusion layers let video frames attend to concurrent audio, bringing cross-modal fusion and temporal alignment into perception itself. OmniSchema unifies the outputs of all ten parsing tasks, allowing a single self-evolving post-training procedure, OmniSchema-Aware On-Policy Distillation (OSAD), to provide a shared token-role-based objective across tasks. In each round, the model is trained on its own rollouts against token-level targets from a reference-conditioned copy of itself, which is then refreshed with the trained weights, so that the parser and its supervision co-evolve. With a single 5 B-parameter checkpoint, Youtu-Parsing-Omni achieves state-of-the-art performance on OmniDocBench v1.6 (96.96 Overall) and leads all open-weight models on OmniParsingBench (75.08 Avg.), with its advantage concentrated on audio and video. It also achieves the best overall chart-parsing score (95.02) and the highest exact chemical-fingerprint agreement among compared systems, and matches a dedicated optical-music model in character-level transcription of typeset sheet music while staying far ahead of general-purpose systems. Code and model weights are publicly available.

### [Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38566v1)

- **Authors:** Chengsheng Zhang, Wenshen Xu, Yuchuan Wu, Zhiyuan Yu, Junfeng Fang, Tianlong Wang, Bingxue Qiu, Yongbin Li, Xiang Wang
- **Published:** 2026-10-28
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38566v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38566v1)

Rubric-based reinforcement learning (RL) offers interpretable, fine-grained supervision for aligning large language models (LLMs) on open-ended generation tasks. However, static rubric criteria may become saturated or remain beyond the policy's exploration, rendering the reward signals insufficiently discriminative. While recent efforts seek to guide sampling exploration or evolve rubrics, they fail to explicitly factor in how rubric difficulty should track the evolving policy's capabilities, resulting in a persistent mismatch between the optimization objective and the policy's current capability frontier. In this paper, we propose \textbf{Co}-Evolving Policy and Rubric via \textbf{C}apability \textbf{A}wareness (\textbf{CoCA}), a closed-loop framework that jointly adapts the rubric and policy through criterion-level diagnosis. Specifically, CoCA contrasts query-only and rubric-guided rollouts to characterize each criterion relative to the current policy, recalibrates criteria with mismatched difficulty, and identifies guidance-revealed capability gaps for frontier-based learning. Based on this insight, CoCA either expands rubric-conditioned frontiers through guided optimization or transfers qualified gains through on-policy self-distillation. As the policy updates, CoCA improves the capability diagnosis and enables more reliable distillation, thereby completing the co-evolution loop. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and LLMs demonstrate that CoCA consistently improves performance over rubric-based RL methods. Moreover, CoCA promotes the internalization of guidance-revealed capabilities into query-only behavior while mitigating capability degradation.

### [ParetoRSI-Gym: Can AI Agents Improve Language Models Across Multiple Domains?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386v1)

- **Authors:** Haojin Wang, Yangzhen Wu, Zeyi Chen, Jian Xie, Jiaying Ye, Lili Meng, Shuyun Tang, Shangding Gu, Dawn Song
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34386v1)

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) requires an agentic system to design, train, and evaluate its own successor. Data-driven post-training is a central part of that loop, yet existing evaluations measure it on a single benchmark, where an agent selects from a static pool or distills a stronger model to push one score up in one run. Real post-training is multi-domain, and a gain in one domain has to be weighed against a regression in another. We propose ParetoRSI-Gym, in which an agent searches for data, curates it, trains, reads back per-domain results, and repeats, with everything but the data held fixed: the initial LLM, the five domains (math, code, finance, medicine, science), the toolset, and the SFT pipeline. Generalization benchmarks in each domain separate capability gains from overfitting to the scores the agent optimizes against. Across three agent systems, three base models, five rounds and multiple seeds, the strongest system, Claude Code with Opus-5, raises the generalization scores of its proxy-best checkpoint by 9.2% relative to the starting model (+2.3 absolute points), averaged with equal weight over three base models and five domains, with a single-domain relative gain of up to 63% (medicine on Ministral-3-3B). Further analysis shows that the harness matters as much as the model it drives, and that each agent keeps a unique data selection strategy. We see our work as a step towards evaluation of RSI systems in real-world machine learning engineering.

### [StateWise: Teaching Language Models to Track and Obey Evolving Constraints in Multi-Turn Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33766v1)

- **Authors:** Samarth Varshney, Ritu Yadav
- **Published:** 2026-10-24
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33766v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33766v1)

Users of conversational assistants rarely state all requirements up front: they add constraints, amend them, withdraw them, digress, and return. Instruction-tuned LLMs fail here in two ways: active constraints stop being honored as turns accumulate (persistence failure), and withdrawn constraints keep being enacted (relapse). The only existing repair we know of is an external ledger that re-injects a compiled specification every turn. We propose StateWise, a self-improving approach that teaches a single LLM to maintain its own revisable constraint state, a typed state with tombstones for revoked constraints, so persistence and relapse are measured by executable checkers and ground-truth states are compiled for free. StateWise fine-tunes the LLM on two complementary skills, summarizing a dialogue into its constraint state and responding conditioned on that state, then self-trains on its own trajectories, keeping only turns that pass an exact round-trip check against the compiled state. This teaches 7-8B models to track the state almost perfectly but, surprisingly, not to obey it: on Llama-3.1-8B every supervised variant roughly doubles relapse. We therefore add a preference stage on self-generated relapse contrasts, where the rejected response enacts a revoked rule and the chosen one passes the round-trip check. On two backbones with held-out constraint families and unseen phrasings, StateWise cuts relapse by 8-22 points relative to supervised self-training, lifts conversation success by 8-19 points over the backbone, and beats an oracle compiled-ledger baseline on relapse with no inference-time scaffolding. We also characterize the recipe's cost on general instruction-following benchmarks and study weight interpolation as a mitigation.

### [On-Policy Self-Training: Data-Free Self-Improvement in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770v1)

- **Authors:** Leon Sick, Lukas Hoyer, Yannick Strümpler, Christian Reisswig, Timo Ropinski, Federico Tombari
- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32770v1)

On-policy self-distillation enables post-training foundation models without relying on stronger external teachers. However, existing approaches rely on ground-truth question-answer pairs, human-curated problem sets, or other supervised signals. In this work, we reformulate on-policy self-distillation as a self-training framework, driven by self-generated question-answer pairs and privileged reasoning demonstrations. We push supervision to the limit: guided solely by coarse, high-level topic descriptions, the model synthesizes its own curriculum of queries and privileged reasoning traces without access to any human-curated prompts or answers. We find that self-distillation on fully synthetic privileged information enables a model to improve across multiple domains while retaining its capabilities on domains it is not self-trained on. We demonstrate the generality of this framework across text and multimodal domains, achieving consistent self-improvement on standard language benchmarks and Visual Question Answering. Our findings demonstrate that foundation models possess rich latent capabilities that can be amplified without external task supervision or curated problem distributions.

### [Self-Improving Training & Thinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21343v5)

- **Authors:** Ellen Xiaoqing Tan, Jack Lanchantin, Zichun Yu, Polina Kirichenko, Shehzaad Dhuliawala, Danwei Li, Thao Nguyen, Jing Xu, Ping Yu, Ilia Kulikov, Sainbayar Sukhbaatar, Jason Weston, Xian Li, Olga Golovneva

- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a

- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21343v5) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21343v5)

Large language models are classically trained in stages: pretraining on raw text followed by post-training for instruction following and reasoning. However, this separation creates a fundamental limitation: many desirable behaviors such as safety, factuality, overall generation quality, and reasoning ability are only added at a late stage, even though the patterns learned earlier strongly shape a model's capabilities. To tackle this issue, we introduce a new way to pretrain and mid-train models that incorporates these behaviors earlier. We further improve reasoning at all stages by introducing the concept of self-improving post-thinking: improving the model's reasoning and self-judging ability by reasoning after generation. In our experiments, we show these approaches can give strong gains across multiple axes.

### [daVinci-Prover: Agent-Native Mid-Training for Formal Theorem Proving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16433v1)

- **Authors:** Wenhong Zhu, Xuefeng Li, Jiashuo Zhang, Yu Chen, Tiantian Mi, Qipeng Guo, Pengfei Liu

- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL

Advances in large language models (LLMs) are rapidly transforming formal theorem proving. Yet further progress remains constrained by the lack of a transparent and effective data recipe for systematically improving theorem-proving models. In this work, we argue that effective agentic provers require strong mathematical skill priors to ground theorem proving and agentic trajectories to enable long-horizon, interactive proof construction. Computer-use agents (CUAs) have recently achieved superhuman performance on several long-horizon benchmarks. ### [Is Your Long-Horizon Benchmark Really Deep? Measuring and Synthesizing Depth in Terminal Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01927v1)

- **Published:** 2026-11-01
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01927v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01927v1)

Long-horizon terminal tasks are used to evaluate and train agents. Human-curated datasets are scarce, so many synthesis methods have emerged. They usually measure the length of a task by the turns a model needs to pass it, and its difficulty by labels the LLM sets during generation. We find, however, that the turn count largely reflects the habits of the model, not only the demands of the task, and that the labels barely track how often models pass. We describe a terminal task by its work, the number of operations it requires, and its depth, the number of turns that must happen in sequence because the commands of each turn can only be chosen after reading the output of the previous one. Fine-tuning Qwen3-8B on runs from these tasks improves it on two terminal benchmarks.

### [SCOPE: Scene-graph Construction Optimized by Plan Error-correction for Robot Long-term Planning Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31518v1)

Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) stands as a fundamental challenge in robotics, essential for enabling autonomous agents to execute complex, long-horizon tasks in diverse environments. While Large Language Models (LLMs) introduce a new paradigm for TAMP by leveraging extensive common sense, their inherent reasoning limitations hinder direct application, and fine-tuning strategies to enhance these capabilities are currently bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality datasets. Existing automated generation methods often suffer from homogeneity, limiting model generalization, and unsolvability, where hallucinated scene graphs create physical mismatches with task goals. ### [AgentSnare: Learning to Delay, Divert, and Defuse Autonomous Web Penetration Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26998v5)

Large language model agents automate web penetration testing through observation-action loops, enabling defenders to influence decisions with deceptive decoy observations.
