## Recursive Self-Improvement

### [Beyond Self-Consistency: Recursive Self-Improvement at Test Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965v1)

- **Authors:** Jianyu Wu, Yizhou Wang, Encheng Su, Jinouwen Zhang, Pengze Li, Lintao Wang, Chen Tang, Aoran Wang, Wanli Ouyang, Shixiang Tang
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30965v1)

Large language models (LLMs) that continue improving after deployment (e.g., recursive self-improvement) offer a promising path toward general intelligence. Current test-time methods pursue this goal on unlabeled questions, primarily relying on self-consistency: they generate independent solutions and use majority voting to select an answer or construct training targets. This leaves earlier reasoning unused at inference and can reinforce an incorrect majority during training. Humans instead build on earlier attempts to preserve useful reasoning, correct mistakes and improve their problem-solving strategies. Following this principle, we propose Test-Time Recursive Self-Improvement (TT-RSI), a label-free framework for improving LLMs at test time through two mutually reinforcing components: (1) In-Context RSI repeatedly re-solves each question using the full previous solution as a reference, letting the model decide whether and how to use it so that inference compute builds on earlier reasoning. (2) In-Weight RSI distills successive refinements into the model's weights, enabling progress beyond an initially wrong majority and theoretically bringing the first attempt toward the model's infinite-step refinement limit. The updated model answers better and refines more effectively, generating stronger references for subsequent training. Extensive experiments show that TT-RSI improves average accuracy by +13.89% over the base model and outperforms the strongest self-consistency-based method by +4.79% under the same test-time scaling budget.

### [Beyond Weight Updates: Evolving Agent Harnesses for Domain Specialization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25380v1)

- **Authors:** BoHao Chen
- **Published:** 2026-11-19
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25380v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25380v1)

Domain specialization of LLM agents is usually framed as a weight-update problem: collect trajectories, run reinforcement learning (RL) or supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and deploy a new checkpoint. We study a complementary axis: evolving the \textbf{agent harness}---the system prompt, tool schemas, skill library, and planning scaffolding---while keeping the policy frozen. We cast harness evolution as an RL-style outer loop and instantiate it with GEPA, a reflective evolutionary optimizer. The resulting harness adapters require no GPU training, are loadable per vertical domain, and compose with weight-based RL. Experiments with Codev, a Claude Code-style coding agent, on SWE-bench Verified, OfficeBench, and Terminal-Bench show a consistent pattern: weight updates give the strongest single-path gains, harness evolution recovers most of those gains at a small fraction of the compute, and combining both paths is best overall. We also analyze a failure mode of SFT-based specialization---shortened reasoning chains and increased truncation---and argue that harness adapters avoid it by construction. Our results position harness evolution as a practical, non-destructive complement to weight updates for vertical agents.

### [RSI-Prime: Training Models to Train Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138v1)

- **Authors:** Dewu Zheng, Yang Ye, Hongyu Zhang, Jun Yu, Yanlin Wang
- **Published:** 2026-11-17
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23138v1)

Enabling large language models (LLMs) to autonomously conduct training and improve target models is an important foundation for recursive self-improvement (RSI). As LLMs expand their role from being trained to conducting training, their model-training capability warrants dedicated study and systematic development. To this end, we construct RSI-Prime, a dataset for improving model-training capability, comprising 350 complete experimental trajectories across seven task categories. To help LLMs learn to make high-quality experimental decisions from training practice, we perform fine-grained decision identification and quality annotation, then apply decision-level loss masking to exclude supervision on low-quality behavior. Experiments with GLM-5.3 on PostTrainBench, a benchmark for autonomous post-training, show that training on RSI-Prime yields a 31.95% relative improvement in overall performance over the original GLM-5.3, highlighting a path toward future RSI systems in which models autonomously sustain their own advancement.

### [CO-GRAFT: Attributing Reinforcement Learning Gains in Search Agents via Role-Level Policy Grafting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31185v1)

- **Authors:** Ramraj Chandradevan, Sayontan Ghosh, Vinoth Selvendran
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.IR, cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31185v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31185v1)

Reinforcement learning (RL) improves large language model search agents by up to 32 exact-match (EM) points over their pre-RL initializations, yet overall accuracy gains do not identify which underlying functional capabilities improve or interact during training. We introduce CO-GRAFT, a reward-agnostic interventional attribution framework in which an RL-trained agent and its pre-RL initialization jointly generate a single trajectory under closed-loop execution with live retrieval. Each policy controls a mutually exclusive subset of four functional roles: intermediate reasoning, search-or-answer decision, search query generation, and final answer extraction. Evaluating all 2^4=16 role assignments without modifying weights or retraining defines a cooperative game whose Shapley values decompose the total RL gain across roles, while M"obius interaction terms distinguish standalone role improvements from multi-role coupling. Applying CO-GRAFT to Search-R1 agents across Qwen2.5 and Llama-3.2 models (spanning two sizes, base and instruction-tuned initializations, GRPO and PPO optimizers, and NQ and HotpotQA datasets), we show that query generation never exceeds 15% of the total gain; instead, gains are governed by pre-RL answer validity, search retention, model scale, and fixed task-level search budgets, shifting credit from reasoning toward answer extraction in larger or well-formatted answer response checkpoints while smaller base models rely on reasoning--decision coupling.

### [Precision-Aware Learning Rate Transfer Across Block Count in LLM Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31166v1)

- **Authors:** Nan Chen, Soledad Villar, Soufiane Hayou
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31166v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31166v1)

The post-training of pretrained large language models, including supervised finetuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning with verifiable reward (RLVR), is very sensitive to the learning rate, and tuning it through grid search is costly in both computation and memory. In this work, we propose \textbf{TipSweep}, a method to transfer the learning rate across the number of trainable transformer blocks. To do so efficiently, we search for the best learning rate by training only the last $k_0$ blocks (in our experiments, typically $k_0=1$), and provide scaling rules that predict the optimal learning rate when training any set of $k$ blocks, including when $k=L$ (the full transformer). Across experiments with models ranging from 1 to 7 billion parameters, we observe that the optimal learning rate follows precision-dependent scaling rules: the optimal rate scales approximately as $k^{-1}$ with float32 (FP32) master weights, but as $k^{-1/2}$ when updates are applied directly to bfloat16 (BF16) weights without a master copy. We explain both rules using an analytical model that accounts for update rounding, under assumptions supported by profiling experiments. Finally, we develop two prototypes of the learning rate search for a 6.7-billion-parameter model. The first fits the search on a single consumer GPU with 16 GiB of memory. The second speeds up the search on a high-end GPU by evaluating all candidate learning rates in a single pass over the data, so that a five-candidate search takes about half the GPU hours of one full-depth ($k=L$) run.

### [Lowering the Bar for Everyone: Hidden Validation-Seeking Goals in Empathetic RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30702v1)

- **Authors:** Xinche Zhang, Shanghong Xie, Zhiyuan Ma, Jinhao Li, Sen Song
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30702v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30702v1)

When RL learns from the reactions of many users, the policy usually cannot see what each user wants. We ask what it learns from users who privately want validation: to validate them alone (targeting), or to lower its bar for endorsing everyone (a threshold shift). We answer this in one setting, empathetic RL against LLM-simulated users (RLVER, GRPO, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct): we vary only the share of validation-seeking users (0--50%; RLVER's default: 24.6%) and evaluate on 1,000 Reddit AITA posts with community fault verdicts. The policy shifts its threshold. Endorsement of at-fault posters rises by 3--4 percentage points per 10 points of share; without such users the Qwen model stays at base, and the effect persists at matched KL and under a goal-blind reward. Signal-detection analysis shows that the criterion for endorsing falls while discrimination between at-fault and not-at-fault posters does not decline. An explicit request to be told if one is wrong gives no measurable protection: at-fault posters who make it receive nearly the same rise as those who do not (+0.11 vs. +0.13). When the goal is stated during training, the policy instead largely targets, endorsing seekers regardless of fault. Standard sycophancy and empathy benchmarks miss the shift; five of six usable released empathetic-RL checkpoints also endorse at-fault posters who ask for honesty more than their base models, though a pre-registered prediction for two held for only one. The user mix is a training hyperparameter to audit and report.

### [From Rewarding What Models Say to How They Think: Attention Process Reward Model for LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30627v1)

- **Authors:** Kairong Han, Nuanqiao Shan, Ziyu Zhao, Xinpeng Dong, Zijing Hu, Dian Jin, Leyan Deng, Junjian Ye, Lujia Pan, Fei Wu, Kun Kuang
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30627v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30627v1)

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become an important component in large language models (LLMs). However, existing RL approaches focus on final outputs or textual solution trajectories, leading to coarse-grained rewards and unreliable reasoning. Such paradigms focus mainly on what the model outputs while overlooking how the model internally reasons. Therefore, can we reward how a model thinks internally rather than merely what it says? We first reveal the problem of textual reward aliasing in existing reward modeling approaches. Motivated by this, we propose the Attention Process Reward Model (APRM), which assigns internal rewards based on hidden states and attention maps to capture high-quality reasoning patterns. With only approximately 46M parameters, APRM is trained using ranking and counterfactual objectives, and its reward guides RL optimization while being periodically updated to handle distribution shifts. Integrated with representative RL methods, APRM achieved an average pass@8 gain of 2.88\%. Our results demonstrate the promise of incorporating internal reasoning signals into RL for LLMs.

### [Auxiliary Clues Aware's Geometry Problem Solving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30617v1)

- **Authors:** Qigong Lei, Qingsong Wang, Wang Lin, Qianyi Yang, Chang Yao, Jingyuan Chen
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30617v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30617v1)

Geometry Problem Solving (GPS) serves as a rigorous test of the complex reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly when solutions require auxiliary constructions absent from the input diagram. However, explicit diagramming methods suffer from unstable image generation quality and cascading errors, while existing implicit attention mechanisms primarily focus on visible elements, limiting their ability to support reasoning that requires additional geometric constructions. To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{AuxCA} (Auxiliary-Clues-Aware GRPO), a reinforcement learning framework that helps models learn transferable auxiliary-construction strategies that can be applied beyond the training domain without explicit diagram generation at inference time. Specifically, we introduce Auxiliary Clues Dependency (\textbf{ACD}) to quantify the influence of auxiliary visual clues on token generation and integrate it into GRPO through fine-grained advantage weighting. Furthermore, we incorporate an auxiliary incentive reward to encourage the effective use of auxiliary geometric clues in correct solutions. Extensive experiments demonstrate that AuxCA-8B outperforms the explicit-plotting baseline Bagel-Canvas and achieves competitive performance with leading proprietary models on auxiliary-dependent plane geometry problems. Improvements on analytic geometry, solid geometry, and external benchmarks further suggest that the learned strategies can transfer beyond the training domain. Code is available at https://github.com/fukua23/AuxCA

### [SceneCast: Learning Indoor Scene Synthesis from Curated Synthetic Videos with LLM Reward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30345v1)

- **Authors:** Ziyang Zong, Canming Xia, Haoran Xu, Guang Tan
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30345v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30345v1)

3D indoor scene synthesis requires generating objects to form a complete and coherent scene. Existing approaches rely heavily on manually annotated datasets, leading to high costs and limited generalization. We present SceneCast, an annotation-free framework that trains on large-scale, unlabeled videos, addressing two key challenges: (1) Videos provide only partial views of a scene, requiring the model to extrapolate unseen 3D regions, which is inherently error-prone; (2) Raw videos available online are uncurated, suffering from uncontrolled conditions and variable data quality. First, we design a Transformer-based model that predicts objects sequentially and is fine-tuned with reinforcement learning (RL), where an LLM acts as a spatial common-sense evaluator. The LLM provides reward signals through chain-of-thought reasoning and code-executable geometric checks to refine object configurations. Second, we introduce \trim, a data curation approach that generates training videos with controllable video generation models. Crucially, it retains only high-quality prefix clips via multi-criteria quality assessment with an entropy-based weighting strategy. Evaluated on curated synthetic video sets, our annotation-free approach outperforms state-of-the-art supervised methods in scene quality.

### [SAEPO: Sparse-Autoencoder-Guided Credit Assignment for Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29936v1)

- **Authors:** Yusong Zhao, Zihao Zhao, Zirui Zhang, Tunyu Zhang, Ziyan Wang, Yi Wang, Chengzhi Mao, Hao Wang
- **Published:** 2026-11-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29936v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29936v1)

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has demonstrated strong performance in improving large language model (LLM) reasoning, yet its credit-assignment signal remains coarse. Outcome verifiers provide scalable supervision by judging whether a completed response is correct, but they do not directly indicate which intermediate reasoning tokens contribute to success or failure. As a result, policy optimization can be inefficient, especially when training base models directly from sparse outcome feedback. In this paper, we introduce SAEPO, a Sparse AutoEncoder-guided token-level credit assignment method for outcome-supervised Policy Optimization. Rather than treating correctness only as a rollout-level (sequence-level) signal, SAEPO uses a pretrained sparse autoencoder (SAE) to build token-level semantic representations within reasoning trajectories; it scores each token based on the SAE concepts (e.g., penalizing wrong "final-answer" tokens but rewarding correct "reasoning-process" tokens), learned in an unsupervised manner, to achieve fine-grained credit assignment. SAEPO is a plug-in advantage-shaping module compatible with various policy optimization algorithms. We instantiate SAEPO with both GRPO and GSPO, covering token-ratio and sequence-ratio policy-gradient objectives, and evaluate SAEPO across three LLM backbones on six math and two code benchmarks. Empirical results show that SAEPO improves both the math and code benchmark averages over the matched baselines, with gains of up to 6.63% and 5.57%, respectively. On Qwen3-1.7B-Base, training curves also show faster early accuracy growth. Feature and token-level analyses suggest that the SAE-derived signal favors question-relevant concepts and reasoning tokens.

### [Dimension Is Free, Horizon Is Not: When Evolution Strategies Rival Policy Gradients for LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29795v1)

- **Authors:** Qiyao Liang, Kajetan Schweighofer, Xin Qiu, Risto Miikkulainen
- **Published:** 2026-11-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29795v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29795v1)

Policy gradient (PG) methods such as GRPO dominate LLM fine-tuning, yet recent work showed that Evolution Strategies (ES) also scale to fine-tuning models with billions of parameters. Which method needs fewer samples, and on which tasks, remains unclear. We view the two methods as estimators of the same gradient that differ in where they explore. Because ES perturbs parameters, its noise grows with the number of parameters, and because PG samples actions, its noise grows with the number of generated tokens. Only noise along the directions that change the reward slows learning. The cost of ES then depends on the effective dimension, the number of independent skills a task requires rather than its parameter count, while the cost of PG depends on the effective horizon, which counts only the tokens that share both credit and weights with the reward-carrying ones. We train both methods on a synthetic task where the number of skills and the effective horizon can be varied separately. - **Published:** 2026-11-17
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.IR, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22838v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22838v1)

Generative recommenders decode the semantic ID of a user's next item with a language model, and post-training them with reinforcement learning (RL) after supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has become standard practice. The gains are mostly reported on the first ten results, yet in deployment such models hand hundreds of candidates to a downstream ranker or reward model. To our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of this recall loss: we measure it, identify its cause, and show that, once the cause is known, a simple remedy suffices.
