### [Self-Improving Agents with World Model Predictive Paths](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28945v1)

- **Authors:** Haori Lu, Tianyu Gao, Yaoyao Liu, Yingying Li
- **Published:** 2026-10-20
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28945v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28945v1)

Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly expected to interact with environments and improve their decisions during deployment. However, how anticipating future outcomes and drawing on accumulated interaction experience can jointly support decision-making remains underexplored. We propose a test-time framework, inspired by Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, that combines multi-branch predictive analysis with experience-based adaptation. At each step, the agent proposes multiple candidate actions and imagines their outcomes with a world model, so that alternatives can be compared without being executed in the environment. Since language environments provide neither explicit dynamics nor costs, the predicted outcomes are evaluated for task progress and risk, and MPPI-style weights over the resulting costs guide the final decision. A dual-branch experience memory enables self-improvement: the planner branch refines candidate generation and action selection, while the world model branch improves next-observation prediction. Experiments on ALFWorld and ScienceWorld across three backbones demonstrate the strong overall performance of our method and the effectiveness of experience-guided predictive control. Our code will be made publicly available.

### [RSI Control: Can AI Models Perform Covert Sabotage in the Self-Improvement Process?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27735v2)

- **Authors:** Kaiwen Zhou, Udari Madhushani Sehwag
- **Published:** 2026-10-20
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27735v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27735v2)

Recursive self-improvement, in which frontier AI models work on training the next generation, is quickly becoming a reality. However, the safety of this process has received far less attention than it should. A misaligned model training its successor can shape what that successor knows, how it behaves, and what it wants, and the harm carries forward into the next generation. We study this risk through the lens of AI control. We introduce RSI Control Arena, a testbed in which a frontier model, acting as an autonomous R\&D agent, is given a benign main task from a stage of a successor model's training pipeline together with a covert side task to install a misaligned property in the successor. The testbed contains 16 side tasks in three classes, forming 22 task pairs with main tasks that span the pipeline from data curation to reinforcement learning. We evaluate six frontier models as agents against LLM monitors that read the transcript of the run and the agent's reasoning. We observe that every model completes a substantial share of the side tasks without degrading the main-task performance, including side tasks that make the successor itself a better saboteur. Under minimal instructions to stay covert, models already produce successful attacks that monitors fail to detect, and providing a short list of evasion tactics further reduces the detection rate. Monitors with access to agent's reasoning detects most attacks, but not all. Our results indicate that recursive self-improvement raises significant safety concerns, and that strong safety effort should be in place before models are trusted with greater responsibility of training their successors.

### [Drafting Agents, Not Just Tokens: Speculative Recursive Self-Improvement for Agentic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16491v2)

- **Authors:** Xin Xu, Ke Cheng, Hao Wang, Yangru Huang, Yanghao Xiao, Zhichao Chen, Zhisheng Zhong, Xun Liu, Yi Li, Qian Li, Haibin Lv, Jun Zhang, Jie Jiang, Zhouchen Lin
- **Published:** 2026-10-12
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16491v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16491v2)

Improving an agent through successive revisions requires balancing exploration and evaluation under a limited budget. A narrow search can miss useful sequences of edits, while fully evaluating every intermediate version often exhausts the budget before those sequences yield a verified improvement. Inspired by the draft--verify principle of speculative decoding, we propose Speculative Recursive Self-Improvement (SpecRSI), a two-level framework coupling inner-loop agent search with outer-loop improver learning. SpecRSI drafts successors before their predecessors are fully evaluated, while adopting only fully evaluated, admissible versions. In the inner loop, Verification-Aware Tree Search (VATS) allocates the budget between further edits and outstanding checks using the predicted verified return of a continuation. In the outer loop, Group Verification Optimization (GVO) updates the improver from completed searches while keeping the real evaluator fixed. We evaluate SpecRSI on scientific optimization (including active queue management (AQM) for network infrastructure), coding, and general agent-system benchmarks. Under matched evaluation-call budgets, SpecRSI improves scientific terminal gain over Janus by 60.0%--96.3% and area under the budget curve by 32.1%--91.6%, while achieving higher means across all 50 scientific backbone--task pairs and outperforming MetaRSI-v1 by 1.30--2.70 percentage points (pp) in system score across twenty backbones. On instance-level comparisons, it further improves PyBaMM terminal gain by 142.6% and SWE-Pro success by 6.0 pp at 7.0% and 9.2% lower token cost, respectively. These results demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed approach.

### [EmbodiedRSI: Active Continual Robot Learning Through Hypothesis-Guided Co-Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10498v2)

- **Authors:** Python Song, Zhixuan Liang, Kelsey Fu, Mengdi Wang, Junfeng Yang, Shilong Liu
- **Published:** 2026-10-07
- **Priority:** 📈 Highly cited
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10498v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10498v2)

Robot foundation models provide strong visuomotor control, yet their performance can degrade when object positions or task instructions change. Further improvements often require post-training on substantial robot data, which can be costly to collect through methods such as teleoperation. Agentic harnesses can adapt around the model, but current self-evolving harnesses use robot trials inefficiently when deciding which code and skill changes to pursue. We introduce EmbodiedRSI, a self-evolving agentic harness that autonomously decides where to explore next and turns the resulting physical interaction into improved code and skills. EmbodiedRSI realizes this through a Fast-Slow Dual-System Architecture, in which competing code and skill hypotheses are maintained in a Hypothesis Graph. Value-of-Information Experiment Selection chooses physical experiments that can distinguish these hypotheses. Their outcomes guide Code-Skill Co-Evolution. The Slow System builds Hierarchical Memory, and Reward-Grounded Memory Learning selects effective memory according to their value for later Fast-System improvement. On RoboCasa365, EmbodiedRSI reaches 77.0% overall success and 71.3% on Composite-Unseen, compared with 40.1% for the best baseline. EmbodiedRSI also reaches 86.8% overall success on LIBERO-Pro. Beyond benchmark performance, EmbodiedRSI transfers zero-shot to real-world robot, achieving 71.3% overall success across multiple challenging tasks.

### [Verification and Self-Improvement in Agentic AI: Foundations and Limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10611v2)

- **Authors:** Chien-Ping Lu
- **Published:** 2026-10-07
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CC
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10611v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10611v2)

A self-improving agent can find accepted solutions more often without expanding the tasks its verification interface can accept. We extend task-relative verification with explicit support and transcript interfaces to distinguish search gains from changes in accepted-task coverage. A stage's native reach contains tasks accepted with default support; its closure frontier contains tasks accepted with any support the declared interface permits. A semantic containment criterion identifies the authority or exposure condition that every verified escape must violate, while guard-transfer invariance prevents a change of representation from being counted as a capability expansion. An executable Boolean-program repair agent makes the distinction concrete: over 36,864 paired runs, counterexample feedback reduces verifier calls without changing coverage, whereas an enforced permission change admits a new target with a proof excluding every old-interface program. Exact and uniformly gapped randomized verification share this account; independent majority amplification improves reliability while preserving the accepted language. Separate adaptive-selection contracts state what is additionally required to interpret a proposed successor's score as evidence of deployment improvement. Classical hierarchy results and finite audits provide supporting boundaries. The framework specifies the evidence needed to establish improved search, wider accepted capability, and reliable successor selection.

### [FreeEvolve: Learning to Evolve Beyond Fixed Loops](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09197v2)

- **Authors:** Lecheng Kong, Like Hui, Nikos Kanakaris, Prithwish Jana, Sahika Genc, Narayanan Sadagopan
- **Published:** 2026-10-06
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09197v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09197v2)

Agent evolvers automate the design of the prompts, skills and workflows around language model agents, yet the optimization process they follow is still designed by hand: a fixed search loop decides how candidates are evaluated, which are kept and when the search stops. We propose FREEEVOLVE, which automates this process as well. An environment specifies the goal, target agent, evaluator, data and resource limits; within these limits, the evolver itself decides what to test, how much evidence to collect, which candidates to pursue and when to stop. These decisions follow an editable evolution skill, which we improve through meta-evolution by scoring each candidate skill on the fresh target agent it produces. The optimization process thus becomes a capability learned from experience rather than a loop engineered in advance. On tau3-bench, ARC-AGI-2, ARC-AGI-3 and Terminal-Bench 2.1, FREEEVOLVE controls the evolution campaign by itself, yet improves the primary held-out metric by 13.6 points on average and matches or exceeds hand-designed evolvers. The learned process keeps improving with experience: meta-evolved skills add 6.9 points over the seed skill on fresh target agents, demonstrating transferability across environments.

### [Reliable Self-Evolution with Imperfect Proxy Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02975v2)

- **Authors:** Kangjun Noh, Soyu Kim, Kyungwoo Song
- **Published:** 2026-10-02
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02975v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02975v2)

Large language model (LLM)-based self-evolving search is a promising approach to scientific discovery. However, high-fidelity evaluation of every candidate is prohibitively expensive in some domains. Self-evolving systems in such settings therefore rely on low-cost but imperfect proxy rewards, which may assign high scores to infeasible candidates. These false positives may contaminate both the final output and the feedback used to guide subsequent generations. This motivates statistically calibrated reward intervals for more reliable self-evolving search. We propose Conformal Interval-Driven Self-Evolution (CISE), which constructs candidate-specific reward intervals using conditional conformal inference and iteration-wise online density-ratio estimation. CISE uses conservative interval-based rewards for evolutionary feedback and returns candidates only when all required property intervals lie entirely within their respective feasible regions. We derive fixed-iteration coverage results under explicit assumptions of independence and covariate shift. We evaluate CISE on three self-evolving search tasks in materials science. In our experiments, all candidates returned by CISE are true positives under high-fidelity evaluation, whereas the baselines return more candidates but include false positives. These results highlight the value of a smaller, more precise shortlist when downstream validation budgets are limited. Our repository is available at https://github.com/MLAI-Yonsei/CISE.git.

### [A Comprehensive Survey of Agent Distillation across Models, Artifacts, and Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36630v2)

- **Authors:** Ziluowen Luo, Senzhang Wang, Chaozhuo Li, Jun Yin, Hao Yan, Ming Cheng, Chenxu Wang, Songyang Liu, Litian Zhang, Qiwei Ye, Zheng Liu, Chengqi Zhang, Philip S. Yu
- **Published:** 2026-09-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36630v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36630v2)

Knowledge distillation provides a teacher-student framework for transferring capabilities at reduced cost and has been widely studied for large language models (LLMs). As LLM-based agents move from response generation to sustained, closed-loop task solving, distillation expands beyond model parameters to knowledge retained in reusable resources and execution workflows. This shift from model-level to agent-level knowledge transfer supports efficient deployment, broader capability access, and continual self-evolution. However, existing surveys largely examine model distillation and agent system design separately, limiting comparison of transfer strategies across agent components. To bridge this gap, we formalize Agent Distillation as persistent knowledge transfer between teacher and student agents and present its first unified survey. Distinguishing experience used for transfer from knowledge retained for reuse, we develop a knowledge-substrate taxonomy based on where knowledge persists: models, artifacts, or harnesses. Building on this taxonomy, we analyze cross-substrate transfer paths and hybrid strategies. To assess the resulting capabilities, we review benchmarks and evaluation protocols for retention, causal attribution, and deployed utility without further teacher support. This survey provides a unified basis for studying agent knowledge transfer and developing efficient, adaptable, and self-evolving agents.
