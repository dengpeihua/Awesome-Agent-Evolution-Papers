### [VibeGame: Prompt-to-Game Development with AI-Native Engine and Self-Evolving Adversarial Agent Team](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33106v1)

- **Authors:** Wenbo Hu, Ken Li, Jiazhe Wei, Yukang Cao, Weiyi Hong, Jiayi Dai, Chenjun Bai, Jiajun Liang, Yucheng Liao, Ruichuan An, Zeyu Lou, Haofan Wang, Yueming Lyu, Jingkang Yang, Ziwei Liu, Chenyang Si
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.MA
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33106v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33106v1)

Large language model agents have enabled software development through natural-language interaction, but extending this paradigm to end-to-end game development remains challenging. Building a playable game requires coordinating code, scenes, assets, animations, physics, and configurations, while allowing developers to validate their integrated behavior through interactive testing. We introduce VibeGame, a framework for creating complete 2D game projects from natural-language prompts and optional reference images. Its AI-native game engine represents project state as schema-validated structured text and provides frame-synchronous runtime control, enabling agents to inspect game states, execute actions, and verify behavior at their own pace. Built upon this interface, we design an Adversarial Agent Team of eight specialized agents to separate implementation from review, so that problems are found and fixed through multi-round loops. For each conducted and accepted project, we further introduce a training-free self-evolution mechanism to distill it into reusable skeletons, modules, and collaboration contracts for future development. We evaluate prompt-assets-to-game (PA2G), where agents are given an asset library, and prompt-to-game (P2G), where agents generate their own art. On a GameCraft-Bench subset, VibeGame in P2G consistently outperforms a range of agent harnesses in either P2G or PA2G with the same underlying model, including GPT-6.1-Sol.

### [Large Language Models Meet Bayesian Optimization: Towards Self-evolving Wireless Digital Twins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31192v1)

- **Authors:** Yingzhuo Sun, Haoyun Li, Ming Xiao, Francisco J. Ferrao, Berna Ozbek
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** eess.SY, cs.IT
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31192v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31192v1)

Wireless digital twins (DTs) are increasingly integrated with artificial intelligence to support online modeling, prediction, and network optimization. However, most existing approaches perform adaptation within predefined learning or optimization structures, which may become unsuitable when wireless tasks and operating conditions change. In this article, we investigate the integration of large language models (LLMs) and Bayesian optimization (BO) to extend wireless DTs from online adaptation towards algorithm-level self-evolution. We introduce an LLM-assisted BO framework in which BO provides a stable and sample-efficient optimization backbone, while the LLM determines whether selected BO components should be retained or adapted based on the current task and accumulated optimization experience. In simulations, we apply the framework to Gaussian process (GP) kernel discovery for a multi-user wireless beam-selection problem. Simulation results show that the LLM-discovered kernel improves the final BO performance by 19.7% on the training tasks compared with the strongest standard kernel baseline. The results demonstrate the potential of combining LLM flexibility with structured BO for self-evolving wireless DTs.

### [CANNBot-CAKE: Collaborative Agentic Kernel Evolution for Ascend NPU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31034v1)

- **Authors:** Deng Pan, Xue-Jian Gao, Junyi Fan, Bin Du, Jiasheng Li, Xinxian Chen, Nok Yan Chang, Xinlin Li, Lingchao Zheng, Jun Li, Yuwei Fan
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31034v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31034v1)

Efficient custom operators are essential for modern deep learning workloads, yet developing high-performance kernels for Ascend NPUs remains slow, expert-intensive, and difficult to automate. Although AI agents now approach expert performance on GPU kernel benchmarks, current models remain less capable of generating AscendC kernels for NPUs, potentially reflecting the relative scarcity of relevant training data. We thus present CAKE, a CANN-native system for deployable custom operators that combines adaptive multi-DSL routing, separate Generation and Evolution modes, and reviewed knowledge reuse through CAKE Book and a Self-Evolving Agent. On 53 CANN-Bench v1.1 operators on Ascend 950PR, CAKE's aggregated results achieve $100\%$ case pass rate on the standard set (1,060/1,060) and $98.28\%$ on the hidden set (4,167/4,240), with an overall mean score of 78.63 out of 100 and an average performance speedup of $1.334\times$ over the baseline. Evolution then pushes correct operators further: EngramGateFusion cuts aggregate device time by $46.6\%$ over 20 rounds and roughly halves the mean roofline gap. These results offer a practical path toward closing Ascend's AI-for-kernel capability gap.

### [ADAPT for Science: A Multi-Agent System for Self-Evolving Scientific Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30851v1)

- **Authors:** Zhaoran Feng, Yuchen Ni, Jianan Liu, Lemao Liu, Dejing Dou, Kairui Feng
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.MA, physics.optics
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30851v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30851v1)

Scientific discovery often requires identifying a small number of high-performing designs in vast, constrained search spaces. This is difficult when evaluations are costly, feasible solutions are sparse, parameters are strongly coupled, and physical constraints are strict. Recent AI for AI methods can automatically improve algorithms and optimization procedures. However, general purpose approaches are not designed around scientific priors, physical constraints, and limited evaluation budgets. We introduce ADAPT for Science, a multi-agent framework that treats scientific optimization as two coupled decisions: where to search and how to search. ADAPT uses historical designs to identify promising and diverse initial conditions, progressively concentrates computation on favorable search directions, and evolves the optimization procedure from execution feedback. We evaluate ADAPT on the design of gravitational wave detectors using a differentiable physics simulator and eight unseen detector topologies. Under matched scientific evaluation budgets, ADAPT achieves a mean loss of 0.204 +/- 0.101, reducing loss by 54.9% relative to the best classical optimizer and by 62.2% relative to the best general purpose agentic evolution baseline. ADAPT achieves the lowest loss on all unseen topologies among the evaluated baselines. ADAPT provides a step toward scientific optimization systems that continuously learn from prior experience and their own search outcomes.

### [Agentic Knowledge-Model Co-Evolution via Consistency Regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30790v1)

- **Authors:** Sirui Chen, Zhining Liu, Chao Zhang, Jingrui He, Zheng Li
- **Published:** 2026-10-22
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30790v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30790v1)

LLM agents can evolve by learning from accumulated experience. Existing approaches extract reusable external knowledge, such as skills and memories, from experience to guide subsequent task solving, with some further internalizing this knowledge through model training. However, different ways of analyzing and transforming the same experience can yield knowledge with substantially different utility, even when the resulting guidance appears reasonable. Inspired by consistency regularization, we identify consistent utility gains across different ways of analyzing the same experience, rather than relying on any single way of analyzing it. We introduce SPLICE, a framework for consistency-regularized knowledge-model co-evolution. It constructs evolution views by perturbing how the same experience is analyzed, producing different knowledge candidates. Knowledge updates are then guided by common gain, which accounts for utility gains and their consistency across views. During model evolution, self-distillation internalizes the evolved knowledge alongside reinforcement learning, using knowledge associated with consistent utility degradation as a contrastive reference. The two processes interleave, with model updates driving further knowledge evolution. Experiments across diverse domains show that SPLICE outperforms knowledge evolution and model training baselines by up to 7.1 and 4.6 points in average accuracy, while its evolved knowledge reduces output tokens per correct solution by up to 44% and transfers across model sizes and families.

### [RoleFlow: Self-Evolving Multi-Role Agentic Collaboration via Structural Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30429v1)

- **Authors:** Yi Ge, Pan Wang, Xuehao Cui, Zian Zeng, Xu Hu, Heng Huang, Furong Huang
- **Published:** 2026-10-21
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30429v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30429v1)

Large language model (LLM) based multi-agent systems often rely on predefined collaboration structures, limiting their adaptability to evolving task requirements. Although recent approaches enable dynamic orchestration, trajectory-level rewards they apply assign one outcome to each decision, ofen struggling to identify the contributions of individual structural changes. We propose RoleFlow, a self-evolving multi-role agentic system that refines collaboration structures through feedback-driven atomic edits and on-demand role synthesis. To make structural adaptation learnable, we introduce Single-Edit Counterfactual Policy Optimization (SECPO), which evaluates each edit against its no-edit counterpart at the same chain state and assigns targeted credit to edit-specific tokens. Across seven benchmarks spanning four domains, RoleFlow achieves the optimal average performance, surpassing the strongest competitor by 4.7 points. It leads on all three multi-hop QA benchmarks by 3.2-7.5 F1 points and also shows supremacy on mathematical reasoning, web interaction, and code generation. Further analyses demonstrate cross-benchmark transferability, training-data efficiency, and cross-executor generalization.

### [Consistency Is Not Validity: Self-Rewarded Moral Argumentation Makes Language Models Decisive but Pliable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30349v1)

- **Authors:** Marius De Kuthy Meurers, Aaron Parisi, Bernd Bohnet
- **Published:** 2026-10-21
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30349v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30349v1)

LLMs are increasingly consulted for moral judgment. We aim to improve performance on such tasks by rewarding good arguments, on the premise that arguing well increases the likelihood of judging well. We train a reinforcement learning policy initialized from a state-of-the-art language model in a self-judged tournament with masked thinking: the generated arguments are judged pairwise on argument quality by the policy itself. As an empirical basis, we extract authentic dilemmas from classic novels, using a logic solver to verify that each constitutes a conflict. We show that our judge rates argument quality like human annotators, and the policy then adopts the language register that it rewards. Testing on the external benchmarks MoralChoice, ELEPHANT, and MORABLES, we find that the policy becomes more decisive and invariant to the order in which options are presented, but sycophancy and reframing remain a challenge to validity. Two ablation studies with synthetic dilemmas and unmasked thinking match the reordering gains, but show a weaker register shift and higher sycophancy. We conclude that consistency is not validity: consistency under reordering can be optimized without improving the moral judgment it is meant to signal.

### [Environment Duels: A Microcosm of LLM Co-Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30116v1)

- **Authors:** Shibo Jin, Zhiqiu Xu, Yiling Zou, Shangyu Gong, Mayur Naik
- **Published:** 2026-10-21
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30116v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30116v1)

Through a tangle of real-world interactions, from synthetic training pipelines to data shared on the open web, frontier LLMs absorb what their predecessors and competitors create. How well does each model play its role in this ecosystem, as a creator and as a solver? We introduce Environment Duels, a dynamic agentic benchmark that evaluates both capabilities on interactive environments. Each model writes environments, each with a privileged hint, aiming for tasks that capable solvers fail without the hint and pass with it. Across nine frontier LLMs, solving and authoring prove to be distinct skills: the strongest solvers are not the strongest authors, while smaller models learn more from how others played and write the hardest environments and the most useful hints. Beyond the scores, the pairwise interactions form a microcosm of how models affect one another. Head-to-head outcomes are transitive and so yield a stable ranking; independent authors converge on the same designs; and in a reinforcement-learning study with Qwen3.8-27B, the learner's hint lift on an author's environments tracks how much training on those environments improves it on held-out reasoning and agentic benchmarks, whether the environments are its own or another model's. Each of these patterns has a counterpart in how models shape one another outside the duel. By staging these interactions under controlled conditions, Environment Duels offers a window onto how LLMs teach, challenge and learn from one another. Code is available at https://github.com/LLM-Duels/EnvDuels.

### [HGM-Dual: Lineage-Aware Guided Agent Harness Optimization for Long-Horizon Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30042v1)

- **Authors:** Sudipta Paul, Pengrui Quan, Aounon Kumar, Nicholas Tzou, Vivek Kulkarni, Srinivas Chappidi
- **Published:** 2026-10-21
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.NE
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30042v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30042v1)

Harness optimization, which improves an agent's prompts, tools, and workflows, is an increasingly effective way to enhance agents built on frozen or closed-source LLMs. Leading approaches such as the Darwin--Godel Machine and the Huxley--Godel Machine (HGM) rely on recursive self-improvement and were developed mainly for coding, where the competence needed to improve the agent coincides with the competence being improved. Planning tasks, in which an agent must produce a sequence of tool calls that jointly satisfies interacting constraints, break this alignment. There, open-ended self-modification repeatedly explores ineffective changes and consumes substantial evaluation budgets. We introduce HGM-Dual, a diagnostic-guided framework for budget-efficient harness evolution for planning agents. First, HGM-Dual represents agent evolution as lineage-aware context. This context records what each ancestor changed, which behaviors improved or regressed, and which modifications were ineffective, giving the editor explicit edit-outcome attribution instead of an unstructured archive of past agents. Second, HGM-Dual introduces error-conditioned dual-path child generation. For each selected parent, it generates a general child that preserves open-ended exploration and specialized children that target the harness components most likely responsible for the parent's highest-ranked constraint violations. HGM-Dual outperforms the strongest baseline by up to 14.45% relative improvement in case accuracy while matching HGM variants' final performance with 2x-4x fewer evaluation budget.

### [GuideEvolve: Self-Evolving Agent Framework with Expert Guidance and 3Ts Mechanisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30017v1)

- **Authors:** Meihong Jia, Yasemin Timar, Jonathan Taws
- **Published:** 2026-10-21
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30017v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30017v1)

This study introduces GuideEvolve, a self-evolving agent framework incorporating expert guidance and three autonomous regulatory mechanisms, collectively termed the 3Ts: Think Fast and Slow, Throwback, and Termination by choice. The framework addresses critical limitations in extant self-evolving agent paradigms through dual innovations: (1) a task-specific expert-evaluation-driven evolutionary pipeline, and (2) the integration of 3Ts mechanisms to facilitate cognitive optimization, performance recovery via temporal rollback, and adaptive termination protocols. Empirical validation across the MGSM and MMLU benchmarks demonstrates that GuideEvolve attains performance metrics comparable to current state-of-the-art models (89.8% accuracy versus Gödel Agent's 90.6% on MGSM; 89.1% versus 87.9% on MMLU), while exhibiting notably reduced variance (25-59%) and enhanced robustness. Furthermore, the framework achieves a 100% evolution success rate, superior to the baseline's 82%, diminishes required optimization iterations by approximately 50% (from 30 iterations in baseline to 15.1 and 13.4 across tasks), and provides improved stability for production deployment.

### [Evolving with Open Worlds: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement for Future Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29784v2)

- **Authors:** Zhipeng Chen, Yunsen Liang, Yanxi Chen, Hongkang Zhao, Lingyun Feng, Ruiyang Ren, Guoxin Chen, Jia Deng, Feng Chang, Chuan Hao, Ran Tao, Jian Yang, Ji-Rong Wen, Bryan Dai, Wayne Xin Zhao
