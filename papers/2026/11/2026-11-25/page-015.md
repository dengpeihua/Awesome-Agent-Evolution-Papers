### [Reasoning on the Manifold: Bidirectional Consistency for Self-Verification in Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16565v4)

- **Authors:** Jiaoyang Ruan, Xin Gao, Yinda Chen, Hengyu Zeng, Liang Du, Guanghao Li, Jie Fu, Jian Pu
- **Published:** 2026-04-17
- **Priority:** 🏆 CCF-A: ICML 2026<br>📣 Acceptance first observed: 2026-11-25 (actual acceptance date unverified)
- **Venue:** ICML 2026
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16565v4) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16565v4)

While Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) offer structural advantages for global planning, efficiently verifying that they arrive at correct answers via valid reasoning traces remains a critical challenge. In this work, we propose a geometric perspective: Reasoning on the Manifold. We hypothesize that valid generation trajectories reside as stable attractors on the high-density manifold of the learned distribution, whereas invalid paths exhibit off-manifold drift. To operationalize this, we introduce Bidirectional Manifold Consistency (BMC), a training-free, unsupervised metric that quantifies the stability of the generated sequence through a forward-masking and backward-reconstruction cycle. Empirically, we demonstrate BMC's versatility across the full reasoning lifecycle: (1) in Diagnosis, it serves as a robust discriminator of solution validity without ground truth answer; (2) in Inference, it enables rejection resampling to effectively concentrate computational resources on complex reasoning tasks; and (3) in Alignment, it functions as a dense geometric reward that transforms sparse outcome supervision into fine-grained guidance, empowering models to self-evolve beyond standard baselines. Our results establish intrinsic geometric stability as a robust indicator of correctness for dLLMs.

### [Understanding the Challenges in Iterative Generative Optimization with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23994v3)

- **Authors:** Allen Nie, Xavier Daull, Zhiyi Kuang, Abhinav Akkiraju, Anish Chaudhuri, Max Piasevoli, Ryan Rong, YuCheng Yuan, Prerit Choudhary, Shannon Xiao, Rasool Fakoor, Adith Swaminathan, Ching-An Cheng
- **Published:** 2026-03-25
- **Priority:** 🏆 CCF-A: NeurIPS 2026<br>📣 Acceptance first observed: 2026-11-25 (actual acceptance date unverified)
- **Venue:** NeurIPS 2026
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23994v3) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23994v3)

Generative optimization uses large language models (LLMs) to iteratively improve artifacts (such as code, workflows or prompts) using execution feedback. It is a promising approach to building self-improving agents, yet in practice remains brittle: despite active research, only 9% of surveyed agents used any automated optimization. We argue that this brittleness arises because, to set up a learning loop, an engineer must make "hidden" design choices: What can the optimizer edit and what is the ``right'' learning evidence to provide at each update? We investigate three factors that affect most applications: the starting artifact, the credit horizon for execution traces, and batching trials and errors into learning evidence. Through case studies in MLAgentBench, Atari, and BigBench Extra Hard, we find that these design decisions can determine whether generative optimization succeeds, yet they are rarely made explicit in prior work. Different starting artifacts determine which solutions are reachable in MLAgentBench, truncated traces can still improve Atari agents, and larger minibatches do not monotonically improve generalization on BBEH. We then study these factors jointly in harness optimization on TBLite, evaluated on Terminal-Bench 2.0. We also propose a batch-size and credit-horizon rule that, calibrated on TBLite, transfers to DeepSWE after adjusting to its token costs and comes within 1% of the best configuration tested at each context budget.

### [MemReward: Graph-Based Experience Memory for LLM Reward Prediction with Limited Labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19310v5)

- **Authors:** Tianyang Luo, Tao Feng, Zhigang Hua, Yan Xie, Shuang Yang, Ge Liu, Jiaxuan You
- **Published:** 2026-03-13
- **Priority:** 🏆 CCF-A: NeurIPS 2026<br>📣 Acceptance first observed: 2026-11-25 (actual acceptance date unverified)
- **Venue:** NeurIPS 2026
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19310v5) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19310v5)

Reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning improves large language model (LLM) reasoning using reward signals on sampled rollouts. When reward labels are available for only a subset of rollouts, restricting policy updates to that subset leaves the remaining rollouts unused for policy optimization. Semi-supervised learning propagates labels from labeled to unlabeled samples, but existing methods either diffuse labels with fixed similarity weights or treat each sample independently, ignoring the relations among queries, thinking processes, and answers. We propose MemReward, a graph-based experience memory that propagates rewards to unlabeled rollouts during online policy optimization. MemReward stores queries and the rollouts (thinking processes and final answers) of an initial LLM policy as nodes of a heterogeneous graph connected by similarity and structural edges, and warms up a GNN on labeled rollouts to propagate rewards over this graph. During online RL fine-tuning, unlabeled rollouts are attached to the graph by query similarity, and the policy is updated with ground-truth rewards on labeled rollouts and GNN-predicted rewards on the rest. We evaluate MemReward on 13 benchmarks in mathematics, question answering, and code generation with verifiable rewards, and further with LLM-judge, human-preference, and continuous rewards. With ground-truth rewards on only 20% of rollouts, MemReward achieves 96.6% of the performance of fully supervised training (Oracle) on Qwen2.5-1.5B and 97.3% on Qwen2.5-3B, where it also outperforms label propagation and FixMatch-style self-training. It carries over to Llama backbones up to 8B and closely approaches Oracle on out-of-domain tasks.

### [Oracle-Robust Online Alignment for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20457v2)

- **Authors:** Zimeng Li, Mudit Gaur, Vaneet Aggarwal
- **Published:** 2026-02-24
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, stat.ML
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20457v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20457v2)

We study online alignment of large language models under misspecified preference feedback, where the observed preference oracle deviates from an ideal but unknown ground-truth oracle. The online LLM alignment problem is a bi-level reinforcement problem due to the coupling between data collection and policy updates. Recently, the problem has been reduced to a tractable single-level objective in the SAIL (Self-Improving Efficient Online Alignment) framework. In this paper, we introduce a pointwise oracle uncertainty set in this problem and formulate an oracle-robust online alignment objective as a worst-case optimization problem. For log-linear policies, we show that this robust objective admits an exact closed-form decomposition into the original loss function plus an explicit sensitivity penalty. In the finite-response log-linear setting, we develop projected stochastic composite updates for the resulting weakly convex objective and prove $\widetilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})$ oracle complexity for reaching approximate stationarity.

### [Evolutionary System Prompt Learning for Reinforcement Learning in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14697v5)

- **Authors:** Lunjun Zhang, Ryan Chen, Bradly C. Stadie
- **Published:** 2026-02-16
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14697v5) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.14697v5)

Building agentic systems that can autonomously self-improve from experience is a longstanding goal of AI. Large language models (LLMs) today primarily self-improve via two mechanisms: self-reflection for context updates, and reinforcement learning (RL) for weight updates. In this work, we propose Evolutionary System Prompt Learning (E-SPL), a method for jointly improving a model's experiential context and its weights. In each RL iteration, E-SPL samples trajectories under multiple system prompts in parallel, then uses them to jointly update the weights with RL and evolve the system prompts with self-reflection. E-SPL encourages a natural division between procedural knowledge in weights and interpretable declarative knowledge in system prompts. Most notably, in an easy-to-hard generalization setting (AIME $\rightarrow$ BeyondAIME), E-SPL improves RL success rate from 38.8% $\rightarrow$ 45.1%. E-SPL also improves RL on AIME 2025 (56.3% $\rightarrow$ 60.6%), HMMT 2025 (50.0% $\rightarrow$ 52.7%), and agentic search (44.2% $\rightarrow$ 48.6% on gpt-oss-120b). Across all settings, E-SPL outperforms both RL-only and evolution-only baselines, demonstrating that weight updates and context updates are deeply synergistic and can together yield gains in generalization that neither achieves alone. Code is available at https://github.com/LunjunZhang/E-SPL.

### [Sci-CoE: Co-evolving Scientific Reasoning LLMs via Geometric Consensus with Sparse Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12164v2)

- **Authors:** Xiaohan He, Shiyang Feng, Songtao Huang, Zongsheng Cao, Bin Wang, Bo Zhang
- **Published:** 2026-02-12
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12164v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12164v2)

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated exceptional reasoning capabilities, and co-evolving paradigms have shown promising results in domains with verifiable reward signals, such as code and math. However, in unverifiable domains, such as scientific reasoning, these methods remain fragile due to unreliable solution evaluation and limited diversity in verification strategies. In this work, we propose Sci-CoE, a two-stage co-evolving framework for domains without verifiable feedback. The framework enables models to self-evolve as both solvers and verifiers by transitioning from sparse supervision to unsupervised learning. In the first stage, the model uses a small set of annotated data to establish fundamental correctness judgment anchors for the Verifier. In the second stage, we introduce a geometric reward mechanism that jointly considers consensus, reliability, and diversity, driving large-scale self-iteration on unlabeled data. Experiments on several general scientific benchmarks demonstrate that Sci-CoE enhances complex reasoning capabilities and exhibits strong scalability, facilitating the construction of more robust and diverse evaluation systems. Codes are available at https://github.com/InternScience/Sci-CoE.
