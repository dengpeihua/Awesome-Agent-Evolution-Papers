## Recursive Self-Improvement

### [Self-Evolution Reasoner: Continuous Optimization via Policy-Intrinsic Exploration and Exploitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34137v1)

- **Authors:** Wenhang Shi, Yiren Chen, Zhe Zhao, Jinhao Dong, Wei Lu, Xiaoyong Du
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34137v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34137v1)

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) enables large language models (LLMs) to continuously self-improve their reasoning capabilities. Compared to RLVR's scalar rewards, recent self-supervised on-policy distillation methods leverage denser supervision signals. However, both paradigms suffer from exploration bottlenecks, struggling to escape local optima when the policy fails to sample valid solutions. Relying on external demonstrations can bypass this issue; however, the off-policy nature hinders genuine self-improvement and the mismatched-distribution reasoning can not be fully exploited. In this work, we propose Self-Evolution Policy Optimization (SEPO), a framework that drives continuous reasoning capability evolution via guided exploration and in-distribution exploitation. Specifically, SEPO utilizes labels to elicit correct reasoning from the policy, unlocking the exploration potential. By treating current policy conditioned on this in-distribution reasoning as a self-teacher, SEPO distills the newly discovered solution back into the policy. Through this iterative cycle of policy-intrinsic exploration and exploitation, SEPO achieves robust and continuous capability evolution. Across scientific, tool-use, and complex logical reasoning domains, SEPO consistently achieves the best performance under identical training time or steps, demonstrating superior efficiency and convergence ceilings. Notably, SEPO maintains stable improvements even in scenarios where baselines severely underperform or fail entirely, such as on complex tasks or with small-scale models. Our code is available at https://github.com/Eric8932/SEPO.

### [StateWise: Teaching Language Models to Track and Obey Evolving Constraints in Multi-Turn Dialogue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33766v1)

- **Authors:** Samarth Varshney, Ritu Yadav
- **Published:** 2026-10-24
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33766v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33766v1)

Users of conversational assistants rarely state all requirements up front: they add constraints, amend them, withdraw them, digress, and return. Instruction-tuned LLMs fail here in two ways: active constraints stop being honored as turns accumulate (persistence failure), and withdrawn constraints keep being enacted (relapse). The only existing repair we know of is an external ledger that re-injects a compiled specification every turn. We propose StateWise, a self-improving approach that teaches a single LLM to maintain its own revisable constraint state, a typed state with tombstones for revoked constraints, so persistence and relapse are measured by executable checkers and ground-truth states are compiled for free. StateWise fine-tunes the LLM on two complementary skills, summarizing a dialogue into its constraint state and responding conditioned on that state, then self-trains on its own trajectories, keeping only turns that pass an exact round-trip check against the compiled state. This teaches 7-8B models to track the state almost perfectly but, surprisingly, not to obey it: on Llama-3.1-8B every supervised variant roughly doubles relapse. We therefore add a preference stage on self-generated relapse contrasts, where the rejected response enacts a revoked rule and the chosen one passes the round-trip check. On two backbones with held-out constraint families and unseen phrasings, StateWise cuts relapse by 8-22 points relative to supervised self-training, lifts conversation success by 8-19 points over the backbone, and beats an oracle compiled-ledger baseline on relapse with no inference-time scaffolding. We also characterize the recipe's cost on general instruction-following benchmarks and study weight interpolation as a mitigation.

### [On-Policy Self-Training: Data-Free Self-Improvement in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770v1)

- **Authors:** Leon Sick, Lukas Hoyer, Yannick Strümpler, Christian Reisswig, Timo Ropinski, Federico Tombari
- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32770v1)

On-policy self-distillation enables post-training foundation models without relying on stronger external teachers. However, existing approaches rely on ground-truth question-answer pairs, human-curated problem sets, or other supervised signals. In this work, we reformulate on-policy self-distillation as a self-training framework, driven by self-generated question-answer pairs and privileged reasoning demonstrations. We push supervision to the limit: guided solely by coarse, high-level topic descriptions, the model synthesizes its own curriculum of queries and privileged reasoning traces without access to any human-curated prompts or answers. We find that self-distillation on fully synthetic privileged information enables a model to improve across multiple domains while retaining its capabilities on domains it is not self-trained on. We demonstrate the generality of this framework across text and multimodal domains, achieving consistent self-improvement on standard language benchmarks and Visual Question Answering. Our findings demonstrate that foundation models possess rich latent capabilities that can be amplified without external task supervision or curated problem distributions.

### [Consistency Is Not Validity: Self-Rewarded Moral Argumentation Makes Language Models Decisive but Pliable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30349v1)

- **Authors:** Marius De Kuthy Meurers, Aaron Parisi, Bernd Bohnet
- **Published:** 2026-10-21
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30349v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30349v1)

LLMs are increasingly consulted for moral judgment. We aim to improve performance on such tasks by rewarding good arguments, on the premise that arguing well increases the likelihood of judging well. We train a reinforcement learning policy initialized from a state-of-the-art language model in a self-judged tournament with masked thinking: the generated arguments are judged pairwise on argument quality by the policy itself. As an empirical basis, we extract authentic dilemmas from classic novels, using a logic solver to verify that each constitutes a conflict. We show that our judge rates argument quality like human annotators, and the policy then adopts the language register that it rewards. Testing on the external benchmarks MoralChoice, ELEPHANT, and MORABLES, we find that the policy becomes more decisive and invariant to the order in which options are presented, but sycophancy and reframing remain a challenge to validity. Two ablation studies with synthetic dilemmas and unmasked thinking match the reordering gains, but show a weaker register shift and higher sycophancy. We conclude that consistency is not validity: consistency under reordering can be optimized without improving the moral judgment it is meant to signal.

### [ADReasoner: Acoustic-Linguistic Cue-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Explainable Alzheimer's Disease Detection from Speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34220v1)

- **Authors:** Yin-Long Liu, Yuanchao Li, Jiahong Yuan, Zhen-Hua Ling
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** eess.AS
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34220v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34220v1)

Speech-based Alzheimer's Disease (AD) detection holds significant promise due to its scalable and non-invasive nature. However, current Speech Large Language Models (SpeechLLMs), similar to conventional AD detection systems, still treat AD detection primarily as a simple categorical classification task. This paradigm provides limited interpretability of predictions, while leaving the inherent expressiveness and reasoning capabilities of SpeechLLMs largely underutilized. In this work, we take the first step toward reframing AD detection as a deep reasoning task via Reinforcement Learning (RL). We propose ADReasoner, an RL-enhanced SpeechLLM designed to generate accurate diagnostic predictions with interpretable rationales grounded in fine-grained acoustic-linguistic cues. To achieve this, we first systematically construct ADCoT-7K, an AD speech reasoning dataset combining real data and our quality-filtered synthetic data, where each instance features Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations and detailed captions. Second, observing the weak perception of AD-related acoustic-linguistic cues in current SpeechLLMs, we leverage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to develop a cue-perception-enhanced foundation model, ADReasoner-Base, thereby improving AD speech understanding. Third, alongside two standard rule-based rewards, we introduce a reasoning reward and a reasoning-label consistency reward into Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for RL. Furthermore, a shrinkage mechanism is employed to suppress misleading reasoning signals, while a gating mechanism progressively introduces both rewards to stabilize policy training. Experiments on three AD datasets (Pitt, PROCESS-1, PROCESS-2) demonstrate that ADReasoner outperforms previous state-of-the-art models in both detection performance and explanation quality, advancing AD detection toward interpretable reasoning from speech.

### [Implicit Beliefs as Scalable Process Rewards for Strategic Persuasion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33527v1)

- **Authors:** Minqian Liu, Yuexi Shen, Heajun An, Qi Zhang, Pamela J. Wisniewski, Sang Won Lee, Jin-Hee Cho, Lifu Huang
- **Published:** 2026-10-24
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33527v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33527v1)

Training large language models (LLMs) for multi-turn strategic persuasion faces two coupled obstacles: (i) realistic simulation of psychologically grounded persuadees, and (ii) dense, faithful supervision for credit assignment in strategic dialogues. Outcome-only rewards are sparse and uninformative about which conversational moves drove success, while LLM-judge process rewards are expensive and decoupled from the underlying interaction dynamics. We address both challenges with a unified framework. First, we introduce PersuArena, a persuasion simulation environment that produces realistic, non-monotonic trajectories. Second, building directly on this environment, we propose Process Rewards via Online Belief Elicitation for Relative Policy Optimization (Probe-RPO), an online RL framework that converts the persuadee's internal state into dense process rewards. At each turn, three introspective probes are read from the frozen persuadee's logits, yielding a multi-dimensional reward signal that is faithfully aligned with persuasion dynamics. With extensive experiments on PersuArena spanning healthcare, financial advising, workplace management, education, and personal relationships, we demonstrate that our Probe-RPO achieves the full acceptance rate of 52.5%, compared with the 35.8% for the strongest GRPO ablation and 25.8% for the best off-the-shelf LLM. We also conduct comprehensive analyses on the generalization across persuadee models and the persuasion dynamics across conversations. Our work offers a promising paradigm that can provide effective process supervision for strategic dialogue.

### [A Comprehensive Framework for All Stages of Large-Model Training: From Data and Tokenization, through Pretraining, to Post-Training Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33451v1)

- **Authors:** Xiangjing An, Zhibin Jiao, Aiting Chen
- **Published:** 2026-10-24
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33451v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33451v1)

The capabilities of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) arise from the synergy of the full training pipeline, including data and tokenization, pretraining, and post-training (supervised fine-tuning, preference alignment, reinforcement learning, and continual optimization), rather than any individual stage. Existing surveys mostly adopt a single-stage or single-technique perspective, treating related research as isolated systems without unified stage division or a horizontally comparable basis. This paper proposes a unified stage division and a six-dimensional analytical framework covering data, objective function, optimization strategy, system constraints, evaluation, and risk and governance. We systematically review 395 core references from 2017 to 2026, and present a conceptual map of cross-stage effects: forward constraints of pretraining on post-training, and reverse empowerment of post-training on data and pretraining. We derive three core findings. First, the comparability of scaling laws depends on data quality and mixture, a long-neglected variable. Second, preference alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) and reasoning reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards redefine post-training boundaries, while the Pass@1 versus Pass@K debate remains unresolved. Third, multimodal training poses structural challenges to the pure-text paradigm in tokenizer design, training objectives, and evaluation. The paper provides a cross-stage effects diagram, a method-family map, a practical decision tree, and at least 10 verifiable open problems, with an extensible skeleton for focused sub-surveys per stage. As of the retrieval date, peer-reviewed evidence (A+B) accounts for approximately 79% of the 395 references.

### [How Does Post-Training Shape LLM Value Preferences?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33371v1)

- **Authors:** Yang Liu, Shaoyang Xu, Masahiro Kaneko, Chengzhi Zhong, Wenxuan Zhang, Chenhui Chu
- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33371v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33371v1)

During post-training, large language models (LLMs) learn value preferences from corpora that may contain imbalanced proportions of opinions associated with different demographic groups. Amplifying these imbalances may further underrepresent some groups' opinions. However, how opinion mixtures shape value preferences across post-training paths remains unclear. To answer this question, we conduct controlled experiments using post-training corpora with different opinion mixtures and five post-training paths based on SFT, DPO, and GRPO. We find that post-training amplifies opinion imbalances differently across post-training paths. Once value preferences are amplified, whether further post-training can remove them depends on both the post-training method and the opinion mixture in the corpus. More concerningly, a model's stability under interventions depends on whether further post-training changes its value preference. Moreover, these value preferences are transferred to student models even when distillation uses corpora with no explicit opinion questions. These findings suggest that both the opinion mixture in post-training corpora and the choice of post-training method should be considered when developing, evaluating, and reusing LLMs. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/ku-nlp/llm-value-preferences.

### [Where Rounding Happens Matters: Rounding-Location Alignment Stabilizes LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32951v1)

- **Authors:** Yijing Ke, Tianle Cai, Ruosong Wang
- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32951v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32951v1)

Reinforcement learning for large language models often uses separate systems for inference and training. Even with identical weights and BF16 tensors, implementation differences can make an intended on-policy update off-policy and destabilize training. Existing remedies align the entire inference and training stacks, change global precision, or modify the learning algorithm. We ask whether aligning selected parts of the execution stack can stabilize BF16 RL while other implementation differences remain. We align training-side rounding locations with inference in three regions---RoPE, SwiGLU, and residual RMSNorm---by retaining selected intermediates in FP32 until their BF16 outputs. Across four Qwen-family models, aligning all three regions prevents the collapse observed under the default BF16 baseline; RoPE-path alignment alone also suffices in the studied runs, despite remaining numerical discrepancies. The interventions keep the inference engine, matrix-multiplication and attention kernels, and learning objective unchanged.

### [NgramRL: Accelerating RL Post-Training for Structured Generation via Rollout-Aware N-gram Speculative Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32881v1)

- **Authors:** Nilesh Prasad Pandey, Jangseon Park, Jason Kong, Onat Gungor, Tajana Rosing
- **Published:** 2026-10-23
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32881v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32881v1)

Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves the reasoning capabilities of large language models, but its cost is dominated by autoregressive rollout generation, which can exceed 70% of training time. Existing speculative-decoding methods are poorly suited to this setting: model-based drafters consume scarce GPU memory and may become misaligned as the policy evolves, while existing model-free methods do not explicitly exploit repetition across training epochs. We observe that recent rollouts of the same prompt provide a high-quality, self-updating draft source. Based on this insight, we propose NgramRL, a per-prompt n-gram drafter constructed from bounded recent rollout history. NgramRL requires no auxiliary model, drafter training, or persistent GPU-resident drafter state, while target-model verification preserves the rollout distribution. Existing automated generation methods often suffer from homogeneity, limiting model generalization, and unsolvability, where hallucinated scene graphs create physical mismatches with task goals. To address these issues, we propose Scene-graph Construction Optimized by Plan Error-correction (SCOPE), an automated framework designed to generate diverse and executable TAMP datasets. SCOPE incorporates multi-level diversity metrics to maximize the information entropy of scenarios. Crucially, it introduces a Plan Feedback Error-correction pipeline that utilizes planner execution failures as feedback signals to retroactively rectify logical inconsistencies in scene graphs, ensuring physical feasibility. Experimental results demonstrate that SCOPE exhibits greater robustness to erroneous scene graphs and generates higher-quality data. When utilized for LLM fine-tuning, SCOPE-generated data yield a downstream TAMP model that achieves an absolute 12.33% gain in success rate over its counterpart fine-tuned with data generated by DELTA, a state-of-the-art PDDL-based planner.

With external tools, large language models (LLMs) can tackle complex tasks through sustained environmental interaction.
