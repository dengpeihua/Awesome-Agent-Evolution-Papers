### [A Cross-Scenario Survey and Analysis of Self-Evolving Multimodal Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14256v1)

- **Authors:** Jingjing Jiang
- **Published:** 2026-11-10
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14256v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14256v1)

Recent work shows that multimodal agents increasingly self-improve without recurring human annotation. However, each self-improvement mechanism is designed and evaluated within a single scenario, leaving its preconditions unverified under cross-scenario transfer. This survey investigates the structural source of asymmetric mechanism transfer across four scenarios: multimodal reasoning, multimodal generation, GUI and Web navigation, and embodied manipulation. We first formalize the self-evolving multimodal agent by four criteria and a differentia of self-reference, and adopt the three-stage self-evolving loop of experience generation, feedback acquisition, and update consolidation as our analytical framework to position the scenarios along ceilings on rollout count and feedback fidelity. Comparing self-evolving with fixed loops within each scenario separates the limitations that the scenario imposes from those inherent in the self-evolving design. We then recast each mechanism as a trade-off that raises a scarce factor by presupposing a task-structural resource, such as output equivalence or checkable outcomes, and judge it portable, conditional, blocked, or redundant across scenarios. We attribute the asymmetry to divergent combinations of scarce factors and resources across opposite transfer directions, and identify the matched-budget measurements that could overturn these still untested verdicts.

### [Program-Aided Patchable Skill Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14019v2)

- **Authors:** Jiahao Wang, Yuqi Xiong, Qing Yang, Zhipeng Xu, Zhenghao Liu, Zulong Chen, Yubin Bao
- **Published:** 2026-11-10
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14019v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14019v2)

Agent skills have emerged as an effective means of enabling LLM agents to solve complex, interactive tasks by encoding reusable knowledge without modifying model parameters. Recent approaches further improve skill adaptation by distilling reusable experience from agent execution trajectories and evolving skills based on downstream task feedback. However, existing methods primarily represent skills as natural language documents, intertwining procedural logic with descriptive knowledge and making skills difficult to maintain and evolve. To address this limitation, we propose PASE (Program-Aided Patchable Skill Evolution), a program-aided framework that iteratively evolves skills by associating them with executable programs. Unlike prior approaches that encode trajectory-derived experience primarily in natural language, PASE separates reusable procedural logic into editable programs, enabling targeted updates to executable components while reducing the need to repeatedly revise natural language skills. Extensive experiments across diverse models and benchmarks demonstrate that PASE consistently outperforms all baselines, by achieving over 2.8% improvements. Further analyses show that PASE exhibits greater evolutionary stability and generalisation capability, highlighting the critical role of programmatic knowledge in skill evolution. We will release all data and code via GitHub.

### [WAM is capable in BEHAVIOR-1K: the 2nd-place solution to the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13818v1)

- **Authors:** Yelin He, Jiaquan Ye, Xinming Wei, Wenjie Xiao, Gengming Zhang, Guoting Wei, Xianbiao Qi, Rong Xiao
- **Published:** 2026-11-10
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13818v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13818v1)

This work presents the 2nd-place solution (Team IF1001) to the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge, which evaluates mobile manipulation across 100 household tasks. Our method is built on a powerful world action model (WAM), Cosmos 3. We improve Cosmos 3 by using richer visual history, task-progress conditioning, and task-group and skill-specific LoRA adapters. During inference, we apply a separate progress estimator to supply task-progress context, and use task- and progress-conditioned routing to select a task-group or skill-specific policy, complemented by action-candidate aggregation, rolling inpainting, and task-specific temporal and gripper control. To meet the minimum inference requirement of 1 FPS, we use neither recursive self-improvement (RSI) nor agentic harness methods and rely on no closed-source models; during official private-test evaluation, our system handled concurrent requests across 100 evaluation ports with limited hardware resources, sustaining an average end-to-end inference rate above 2 FPS per port. Our method achieves a mean Q-score of 0.613 and a full-task success rate of 40.9%, averaged over 3 self-evaluation rounds across all 100 tasks. On the official private test set, with 20 instances per task, our system achieves a mean Q-score of 0.615 and a full-task success rate of 41.2%.

### [Toward Autonomous RTL and Synthesis Optimization: Benchmarking and Self-Improving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13713v1)

- **Authors:** Wenji Fang, Yao Lu, Jinghua Wang, Zhiyao Xie
- **Published:** 2026-11-10
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.AR
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13713v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13713v1)

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and autonomous agents are creating new opportunities for hardware design optimization. This paper examines three connected directions toward autonomous RTL and synthesis optimization: (1) Realistic benchmarking. We discuss reference-based and open-ended RTL optimization benchmarks that evaluate optimization opportunities under realistic EDA workflows. (2) Tool-grounded RTL optimization. We present Dr. RTL, a multi-agent framework that automates critical-path analysis, RTL rewriting, and implementation-level evaluation, while continuously improving through reusable optimization skills. Evaluated on 20 real-world RTL designs, Dr.~RTL achieves average WNS/TNS improvements of 21\%/17\% with a 6\% area reduction over commercial synthesis. (3) Design-specific synthesis optimization. We explore agent-driven synthesis script tuning and the discovery of executable synthesis algorithms across intermediate representations, technology-independent optimization, and technology mapping. Finally, we envision AI-native design automation that jointly explores architectural, RTL, and EDA algorithm design spaces, guided by verification constraints and implementation feedback.

### [The RSI Tax: When Recursive Self-Improvement Is Not Worth the Cost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13524v1)

- **Authors:** Yitong Zhang, Ang Li, Yisen Wang
- **Published:** 2026-11-10
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13524v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13524v1)

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) seeks to improve an agent using its own task experience. Its practical value depends on whether performance gains justify the full cost of self-improvement and task execution. We evaluate this trade-off on continual task streams, scoring each task before its feedback is used for subsequent updates. We define the \emph{RSI Tax} as the token cost of the self-improvement process and include it alongside task-execution costs in end-to-end evaluation. Across six methods and two language models, a bounded-note baseline matches or exceeds the observed accuracy of Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, and RRSI, with mean total-token savings of 65.6-71.0\%. We decompose the RSI Tax into verification, exploration, and writing costs, and track subsequent use of the associated state or artifact. For these three RSI methods, verification accounts for 74.8--93.6% of the aggregate RSI Tax. A substantial share of the RSI Tax is also associated with states or artifacts that are not used later in the evaluated streams. These results expose a practical limitation of costly RSI procedures: improvements in the deployed agent need not justify the expense of the improvement process. They motivate RSI objectives that jointly optimize the RSI Tax and expected task-execution cost while ensuring continued growth in agent capabilities across the task stream.

### [Veritas: A Verifier Generation Harness for Agent Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13336v1)

- **Authors:** Daoan Zhang, Qinbo Bai, Dehai Min, Xingyu Mu, Ziyi Chen, Jianing Yin
- **Published:** 2026-11-09
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CR
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13336v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13336v1)

The effectiveness of a self-improving system relies heavily on the quality of its verifier, which judges the outcome of each improvement round. However, the verifiers of most tasks are written by hand, which is expensive and does not scale to tasks that come without one. We propose Veritas, a harness that generates verifiers automatically. Unlike general-purpose agent harnesses, Veritas adds three mechanisms for verifier generation: an evidence header stating what the verifier will observe, a library of domain rules that tasks leave unstated, and authoring principles, partly refined by a self-improvement loop. We evaluate on six benchmarks: four scored by execution, whose official verifiers we first certify, and two that compare pairs of responses. With the model fixed, the verifiers Veritas generates agree better with the official verdicts than those of seven other harnesses on all four execution-scored benchmarks. On the two pairwise benchmarks they are at or near the best. They also agree better than those of the vendor's own harness for two of three other model families. Its mechanisms also improve 9 of 12 harness-benchmark pairs scored by execution across three harnesses.

### [Agentic-OPD: Recovering Forgotten Agent Capabilities via On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12799v2)

- **Authors:** Tingcheng Bian, Kangye Gong, Jinchang Luo, MingQuan Cheng, Feiran Liu, Ni Li, Xiaoling Xia, Haiwei Wang, Hui Li
- **Published:** 2026-11-09
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12799v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12799v2)

Reinforcement learning is now the mainstream paradigm for training LLMs on agentic tasks, yet standard practice keeps only the best-validation checkpoint and discards the rest. We show that this is lossy: in GRPO training of LLM agents, the best-validation checkpoint fails on problems that earlier checkpoints from the same run had already solved, and the union of problems solved across checkpoints far exceeds any single one. The discarded checkpoints thus form a teacher pool obtained at no additional training cost, which on-policy distillation (OPD) can mine to recover the lost coverage. Mining this pool raises two challenges: i) which checkpoint to distill from, as no checkpoint dominates the others and complementarity to the student is problem-specific; ii) where to trust it, as even a well-chosen teacher is only locally superior, so indiscriminate distillation overwrites behavior the student has already mastered. We propose Agentic-OPD, which treats the best-validation checkpoint as the student and all saved checkpoints as frozen teacher candidates, using the student's evolving capability gaps to guide both teacher selection and supervision filtering: the HCN policy (Highest Coverage Next) dynamically selects teachers by weighted coverage of the student's unsolved problems, while the LAD module (Locality-Aware Distillation) confines transfer to failed student rollouts on problems where the teacher performs better. Across five agentic task domains and 24 benchmarks and task subsets, Agentic-OPD improves over its initialization checkpoint by up to 7.4 points, with a 4B student surpassing the latest frontier models GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4.1 on deep search. Agentic-OPD recasts a finished RL run as a reusable pool of policies and establishes post-RL capability recovery as a stage orthogonal to improvements in the RL algorithm itself.

### [SciMARS: Multi-Agent Recursive Self-Improvement for Closed-Loop Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12552v1)

- **Authors:** Weimin Wu, Han Liu
- **Published:** 2026-11-09
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12552v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12552v1)

We propose SciMARS, a framework for recursive task-local adaptation of multi-agent harnesses in closed-loop scientific discovery. Multi-agent systems coordinate analysis, experimental design, and verification as scientific evidence evolves. However, two problems limit their effectiveness: (i) without shared memory, agents lose observations and repeat work; (ii) fixed role procedures, handoffs, and memory policies perpetuate mistakes despite retained evidence. We address these problems with a shared evidence graph and separate component editors. SciMARS adapts roles and coordination; SciMARS + Memory adds a third editor for how agents record, retrieve, and receive evidence. Candidate probes guide composition and selection of the next harness. We evaluate scientific discovery through experimental-data replay and a digital twin. SciMARS + Memory resolves 12/18 GPT-6-Astra and 2/18 GLM-5.3 attempts on Autonomous Science Bench, compared with 8/18 and 1/18 for the base multi-agent team. On ScienceAgentBench and AppWorld, it solves 14/38 and 11/20 tasks, compared with 11/38 and 5/20 for the base team. These results support combining shared memory with harness adaptation across scientific and interactive tasks.

### [ForgeSystem: An Architecture for Demand-Specific Artefact Production with AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12288v1)

- **Authors:** Qingfeng He, Zhui Zhu, Yaojian Chen, Shangzhan Li, Leshan Li, Tingjian Zhang, Qian Xie, Yuxuan Li, Xu Han, Zhiyuan Liu
- **Published:** 2026-11-09
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.SE
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12288v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12288v1)

General-purpose software serves many different demands, but this flexibility costs performance and efficiency on any specific demand. Software tailored to a specific demand avoids these losses, yet developing it requires substantial time and effort from human experts. AI agents offer an alternative: they can design, implement and test artefacts tailored to individual demands. We call this production paradigm Forge. Forging for a demand is economically justified when the cumulative benefit of specialisation exceeds the total cost of construction, verification and maintenance. To make Forge a repeatable production process, we propose ForgeSystem, which consists of a control layer, a forge loop and a recursive self-improvement (RSI) loop. The control layer selects which artefact serves each demand and decides which demands to forge, when to forge them and from which starting point; the forge loop iterates between production and verification until a candidate artefact passes verification; and the RSI loop draws on accumulated production records to supply starting points, refine the harness and train models. We implement ForgeSystem for training frameworks, decoding megakernels and individual computational kernels. Forged training engines reach 1.047-1.332x the model-FLOPs utilisation of their reference frameworks, forged megakernels achieve a geometric-mean decoding speedup of 1.21x over SGLang across thirteen configurations, and forged kernels match or exceed expert libraries in eighteen of twenty-two configurations, while satisfying task-specific quality requirements.
