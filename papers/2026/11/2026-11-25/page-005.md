### [Training LLM Agents to Automate LLM Post-Training with RSI-Dojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03698v2)

- **Authors:** Shuzheng Gao, Zhaojian Yu, Penghao Yin, Yuling Shi, Kai Cai, Shilin He, Michael R. Lyu
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03698v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03698v2)

Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) aims to enable AI systems to autonomously build and improve AI systems themselves, including models, harnesses, and underlying infrastructure. Within this broader agenda, a central capability is enabling agents to train and improve base models. However, existing studies on automated model training primarily focused on workflow- or harness-level optimizations, rather than internalizing the ability to conduct post-training into the model itself. Developing this capability requires executable model training environments that can produce high-quality trajectories demonstrating how to select data, train models, diagnose failures, and iteratively improve results. In this paper, we introduce RSI-Dojo, a collection of executable model training environments designed to teach LLM agents to conduct post-training. To construct RSI-Dojo, we develop RSI-Craft, an automatic pipeline that converts human research artifacts into executable model training environments. RSI-Craft automatically collects research papers from top venues, instantiates datasets and metrics into a unified interface, and reproduces evaluation dependencies. It then filters tasks by data size, execution reliability, and discriminability, yielding RSI-Dojo with 281 seed model training tasks. Building on RSI-Dojo, we employ a trajectory collection method with an improved harness to collect high-quality model-training trajectories from frontier models, then retain examples through rubric-based and outcome-based filtering. We fine-tune Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on these trajectories. The resulting RSI-Dojo-9B and RSI-Dojo-35B-A3B improve over their base models by 8.74 and 16.87 PostTrainBench points, respectively. RSI-Dojo-35B-A3B reaches 29.95 on PostTrainBench and 53.29 on PostTrainBench-Lite, matching the performance of frontier models such as GLM-5.2 and GPT 5.5.

### [Try Before You Evolve: Ranking-Scored Trials for Recursive Self-Improvement of Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39352v1)

- **Authors:** Xu Huang, Jiawei Feng, Jiancan Wu
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.IR
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39352v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39352v1)

Recommenders on Web platforms such as e-commerce and music streaming need their training recipes re-tuned for each dataset. Large language model (LLM) agents can now rewrite these recipes, enabling recursive self-improvement (RSI) of recommender systems. Existing agents propose one edit per round, fully retrain the recommender, and keep the edit only if its ranking quality improves. Unlike programs or patches that can be scored by execution, a recommender edit changes how the model is trained, which raises two challenges. First, ranking feedback is delayed: an edit's ranking quality is known only after full retraining, so much of the retraining budget goes to edits that are later discarded. Second, the signals available before retraining are unreliable: sampled training losses can look solved while full-ranking performance remains poor, and they are not comparable across edits that change the loss or sampler. We propose RecRSI, which generates diverse candidate edits from a diagnosis of the current model and selects one for full retraining using early ranking feedback: each candidate receives a short trial, the first steps of its full training run, scored by full-ranking NDCG@10 on held-out probe users. With two sequential recommenders on three datasets and the same number of full retrainings, RecRSI significantly outperforms existing self-evolving agents in every setting, and on both Amazon datasets reaches in its first round a ranking quality that none of them reaches in five.

### [Youtu-Parsing-Omni: One Encoder, One Schema, All Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39301v1)

- **Authors:** Yunhang Shen, Zhangquan Chen, Xiaotian Li, Zhongpeng Cai, Qing Zhou, Zekai Zhang, Kun Zhang, Zezhong Wu, Jian Hu, Haihan Gao, Heting Gao, Xin Li, Yinsong Liu, Deqian Jiang, Di Yin, Haoyu Cao, Xing Sun, Bing Liu, Yanqiu Qu, Yunfei Wu, Kun Yin, Zhixiang Wei, Yongxiang Hua, Qianyu Li, Xiaoxing Hu, Peiyuan Zhang, Lijiang Li, Dong Xu, Zhenchuan Yang, Yiliang Jiang
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39301v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39301v1)

We present Youtu-Parsing-Omni, an omni-modal parser that converts documents, images, audio, and video into structured records grounded in space and time, providing a perception layer for agents that read, locate, and cite evidence across modalities. Its design rests on one encoder for every modality and one schema for every task. Youtu-Omni-Encoder, initialized from a pre-trained language model to strengthen text reading, encodes all modalities with shared weights in a unified space-time coordinate system, and a few fusion layers let video frames attend to concurrent audio, bringing cross-modal fusion and temporal alignment into perception itself. OmniSchema unifies the outputs of all ten parsing tasks, allowing a single self-evolving post-training procedure, OmniSchema-Aware On-Policy Distillation (OSAD), to provide a shared token-role-based objective across tasks. In each round, the model is trained on its own rollouts against token-level targets from a reference-conditioned copy of itself, which is then refreshed with the trained weights, so that the parser and its supervision co-evolve. With a single 5 B-parameter checkpoint, Youtu-Parsing-Omni achieves state-of-the-art performance on OmniDocBench v1.6 (96.96 Overall) and leads all open-weight models on OmniParsingBench (75.08 Avg.), with its advantage concentrated on audio and video. It also achieves the best overall chart-parsing score (95.02) and the highest exact chemical-fingerprint agreement among compared systems, and matches a dedicated optical-music model in character-level transcription of typeset sheet music while staying far ahead of general-purpose systems. Code and model weights are publicly available.

### [Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38566v1)

- **Authors:** Chengsheng Zhang, Wenshen Xu, Yuchuan Wu, Zhiyuan Yu, Junfeng Fang, Tianlong Wang, Bingxue Qiu, Yongbin Li, Xiang Wang
- **Published:** 2026-10-28
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38566v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38566v1)

Rubric-based reinforcement learning (RL) offers interpretable, fine-grained supervision for aligning large language models (LLMs) on open-ended generation tasks. However, static rubric criteria may become saturated or remain beyond the policy's exploration, rendering the reward signals insufficiently discriminative. While recent efforts seek to guide sampling exploration or evolve rubrics, they fail to explicitly factor in how rubric difficulty should track the evolving policy's capabilities, resulting in a persistent mismatch between the optimization objective and the policy's current capability frontier. In this paper, we propose \textbf{Co}-Evolving Policy and Rubric via \textbf{C}apability \textbf{A}wareness (\textbf{CoCA}), a closed-loop framework that jointly adapts the rubric and policy through criterion-level diagnosis. Specifically, CoCA contrasts query-only and rubric-guided rollouts to characterize each criterion relative to the current policy, recalibrates criteria with mismatched difficulty, and identifies guidance-revealed capability gaps for frontier-based learning. Based on this insight, CoCA either expands rubric-conditioned frontiers through guided optimization or transfers qualified gains through on-policy self-distillation. As the policy updates, CoCA improves the capability diagnosis and enables more reliable distillation, thereby completing the co-evolution loop. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and LLMs demonstrate that CoCA consistently improves performance over rubric-based RL methods. Moreover, CoCA promotes the internalization of guidance-revealed capabilities into query-only behavior while mitigating capability degradation.

### [EvoRouter: Cost-Efficient Multi-Agent Routing via Insight-Guided On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35690v1)

- **Authors:** Yitao Xiao, Xu Zhang, Ye Cheng, Shaoyong Guo, Yan Sun, Ziyue Zhu, Sitao Xie, Guo Tong, Haoran Luo
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.MA
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35690v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35690v1)

Serving web queries at scale with multi-agent systems requires jointly configuring large language models, collaboration modes, agent counts, interaction rounds, and skills. However, the resulting configuration space grows combinatorially, while sparse execution rewards provide limited fine-grained supervision for interdependent planning decisions. We propose EvoRouter, a self-evolving multi-agent routing framework driven by insight-guided on-policy distillation. A dedicated expert periodically synthesizes performance-cost feedback from configuration trajectories across tasks into reusable conditional routing insights about capability requirements, returns from resource scaling, and interactions among configuration choices. A privileged teacher combines these evolving insights with current execution feedback to provide dense token-level supervision for the router's own planning trajectories through on-policy distillation. Cost-aware reinforcement learning jointly optimizes task performance and computational cost. Together, these training signals internalize execution insights into the routing policy. The updated policy generates new trajectories that support subsequent insight extraction, forming an execution-driven self-improvement loop. We evaluate EvoRouter on 19 in-domain datasets spanning five domains and two out-of-domain benchmarks. EvoRouter achieves an average in-domain accuracy of 68.69%, outperforming the strongest routing baseline by 7.23 percentage points while reducing inference compute cost by 50.95%. Our code is open-sourced at https://github.com/CenYangtze/EvoRouter.

### [PhysEvolve: Verifier-grounded Self-evolution for Physical Reasoning with Executable Worlds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34921v1)

- **Authors:** Jinyuan Hu, Yilei Jiang, Chen Tang, Minghong Cai, Xinran Liu, Xiangyu Yue
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34921v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34921v1)

Physical reasoning underpins how embodied systems understand and act in the physical world, yet vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable on questions about dynamics and physical causality. Progress is critically constrained by the scarcity of grounded counterfactual training data, as observational videos capture only the factual trajectory that occurred. The self-evolving paradigm offers a route beyond fixed datasets by enabling models to construct their own training problems. Extending this paradigm to physics, however, requires an external source of truth that can both validate model-proposed environments and verify alternative physical outcomes under intervention. We introduce \physevolve{}, a verifier-grounded self-evolving framework that trains VLMs for causal physical reasoning from model-proposed executable worlds and simulation-authored tasks. At each round, a shared VLM proposes executable world programs and evidence schemas; a deterministic physics engine freezes each valid world, simulates matched baseline and body-elided counterfactual branches, and synthesizes discrete, answer-bound visual tasks. Exact verifier feedback from task solving updates both the shared policy via GRPO and an adaptive curriculum over physical intents. Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, \physevolve{} achieves targeted gains across PhysBench, CausalPhys, and ContPhy without human intervention data, improving PhysBench Throwing by $8.8$ points and ContPhy Counterfactual Removal and Goal-Pit by $7.4$ and $24.4$ points. These gains concentrate on interaction-sensitive and altered-world questions, demonstrating zero-shot transfer of causal principles to unseen complex dynamics (cloth, fluid, soft bodies) and real-world video.

### [ParetoRSI-Gym: Can AI Agents Improve Language Models Across Multiple Domains?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386v1)

- **Authors:** Haojin Wang, Yangzhen Wu, Zeyi Chen, Jian Xie, Jiaying Ye, Lili Meng, Shuyun Tang, Shangding Gu, Dawn Song
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34386v1)

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) requires an agentic system to design, train, and evaluate its own successor. Data-driven post-training is a central part of that loop, yet existing evaluations measure it on a single benchmark, where an agent selects from a static pool or distills a stronger model to push one score up in one run. Real post-training is multi-domain, and a gain in one domain has to be weighed against a regression in another. We propose ParetoRSI-Gym, in which an agent searches for data, curates it, trains, reads back per-domain results, and repeats, with everything but the data held fixed: the initial LLM, the five domains (math, code, finance, medicine, science), the toolset, and the SFT pipeline. Generalization benchmarks in each domain separate capability gains from overfitting to the scores the agent optimizes against. Across three agent systems, three base models, five rounds and multiple seeds, the strongest system, Claude Code with Opus-5, raises the generalization scores of its proxy-best checkpoint by 9.2% relative to the starting model (+2.3 absolute points), averaged with equal weight over three base models and five domains, with a single-domain relative gain of up to 63% (medicine on Ministral-3-3B). Further analysis shows that the harness matters as much as the model it drives, and that each agent keeps a unique data selection strategy. We see our work as a step towards evaluation of RSI systems in real-world machine learning engineering.

### [On the Origin and Evolution of Training-Inference Mismatch in LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40945v1)

- **Authors:** Yu Li, Yufa Zhou, Tian Lan
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40945v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40945v1)

On-policy reinforcement learning for large language models assumes that training responses are sampled from the current policy. In practice, an inference engine samples the responses and a separate trainer computes the gradients, and the \textbf{training-inference mismatch} between the two makes the training data off-policy. The mismatch has two sources, the numerics of the engine and the stale weights of asynchronous sampling, and a correction that stabilizes training under one source can fail under the other. \textit{We ask which property of a mismatch source decides the correction it needs.} Evaluating the trainer at the sampler's weights splits each sampled-token error exactly into a \textit{numerical discrepancy} of the engine and a \textit{policy lag} of the trainer, and we follow both through training. The numerical discrepancy stays approximately constant, so it biases the updates by a nearly fixed amount, and centering the score at each prefix removes its leading bias. The policy lag is produced by the trainer's own updates: the bias it leaves enters the next update, which enlarges the next lag and closes a feedback loop. Importance weighting cuts this loop only while the lag ratio stays concentrated, and on Qwen3-1.7B it fails at the step where the effective sample size of the ratio falls below one half. From this account we derive the \textbf{discrepancy-lag correction} (DLC), which centers the numerical discrepancy, clips the lag ratio against the sampler's weights and resynchronizes the sampler when this effective sample size falls below one half. Under a stale sampler, DLC keeps the KL divergence from on-policy training within twice the KL between two independent on-policy trainings on Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B, and its resynchronization prevents collapses that the per-token corrections leave and that a KL measured on the sampler's prefixes does not detect.

### [CoFiBreak: A Coarse-to-Fine Diversity Reward for Effective Multi-Turn Jailbreak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40926v1)

- **Authors:** Jialin Song, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, Mingqian Feng, Chengquan Guo, Qiyao Ma, Carolyn Buractaon, Anson Ho, Wuyang Chen, Christopher White
- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40926v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40926v1)

Automated red teaming trains an attacker large language model (LLM) to elicit harmful responses from a victim LLM, but reinforcement learning can collapse attackers onto a narrow set of prompts when attack success is the only reward. Such a collapse of attack diversity can bias safety evaluation toward repeatedly testing near-duplicate attack patterns rather than the underlying harmful intents. Existing methods often trade off attack success or inference cost for greater content or strategy diversity. We propose CoFiBreak, a coarse-to-fine diversity reward for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that improves attack diversity while preserving strong attack effectiveness and low inference cost. The coarse reward broadens which strategies the attacker uses by encouraging strategies underrepresented within each GRPO group, while the fine reward broadens how those strategies are realized by discouraging reuse of the same personas, framings, and output formats after harmful-intent information is removed from prompt embeddings. Diversity rewards apply only to attacks that already exceed an effectiveness threshold, so diversity cannot be gained through ineffective attacks. Since both rewards are computed only during training, CoFiBreak also retains inference efficiency. Trained with Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as the victim, CoFiBreak discovers 1.6-2.0x as many distinct successful fine-grained strategies as the strongest baseline across frontier victims on HarmBench. It reaches 98.8% ASR@10 (attack success rate with 10 generations per intent) on AdvBench with the highest diversity score among all compared methods, which is 2.1x that of the strongest-attacking baseline. Sparse outcome rewards make it difficult for long-horizon language-model agents to assign credit to earlier actions. Learned critics provide value estimates for intermediate states, but these estimates can be inaccurate, while estimating intermediate values with repetitive continuation samples from points within a trajectory incurs substantial costs. To mitigate these issues, we introduce FOCUS, an RL method that uses selective branching to help the critic network learn its value estimator. The rollout controller facilitates active learning for the critic by sampling continuations from states that are expected to most reduce the critic's estimation error. ### [BellowsKV: One Latent for Many Layers, Expanded on Demand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35023v1)

- **Authors:** Jiamu Zhang, Hanjie Chen
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35023v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35023v1)

Large language models (LLMs) now serve as the backbone of agents that reason over many turns, call tools, and pursue long-horizon tasks. To avoid recomputation, a model stores the attention keys and values of all past tokens in a key-value (KV) cache. Each layer maintains its own cache, so KV-cache memory can become the main bottleneck for GPU serving. Most compression methods operate within a single layer, by quantizing the cache entries, reducing the channel dimension, or evicting less important tokens. All three lose substantial accuracy at high compression ratios. Cross-layer methods share one compressed cache across adjacent layers to go beyond this limit. However, these methods also degrade sharply at high compression ratios. We find that the KV-cache representations of adjacent layers are quite different, whereas the input hidden states of adjacent layers are highly similar. We also find that, under the same memory budget, reducing the dimension of this shared latent loses far more accuracy than quantizing it. Based on these findings, we propose BellowsKV, a training-free method that maps the hidden states of n adjacent layers to one shared latent per token. At inference time, each layer reads its own keys and values from this latent through a layer-specific projection. We keep the latent at full dimension and compress it in two other ways: BellowsKV-Quant stores the latent at low precision, which is nearly lossless because the latent has no dominant outlier channels; BellowsKV-Evict makes one shared eviction decision per block, because the layers rank token importance very similarly. Across Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, BellowsKV-Quant retains about 99% of full-cache accuracy at 3.6x compression and 97-98% at 13-14x, where the strongest cross-layer method retains at most 84% at 16x. ### [What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491v1)

- **Authors:** Kaiser Sun, Yongchan Kwon, Zain Hasan, Shang Zhu, Kaitlyn Zhou, Federico Bianchi
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34491v1)

Generative models increasingly expose a reasoning effort setting that lets users scale inference-time compute, trading higher cost for potentially improved performance. Users must choose an effort level before knowing whether extra reasoning will help, and even a successful run does not reveal whether the effort purchased was necessary. Reasoning effort is therefore a credence good: buyers observe the outcome but cannot assess the marginal value of the computation they paid for. In this work, we measure the counterfactual effect of effort levels on both proprietary and open-weight models on four benchmarks, spanning single-call reasoning and long-horizon agentic tasks. Effort, it turns out, does not consistently preserve tasks that lower effort solves, can overspend, and can change trajectory behavior. First, we find that although higher effort can raise accuracy in aggregate, it frequently fails on tasks that lower effort solved. Second, on the subset of easy tasks that every effort level solves, higher effort is generally more costly, with the highest effort level costing up to 42x as much as the lowest. Lastly, effort changes not only how many tokens are used, but also how agents solve tasks: at higher effort, agents verify their own work more heavily and more often search for solutions than work through problems directly, both of which lengthen trajectories without necessarily altering the outcome. These findings show that naively choosing the highest level is not only cost-inefficient but also offers no guarantee of the best instance-level performance. A simple alternative, starting at the lowest effort and escalating only after a verified failure, matches or improves on fixed top effort across all models and benchmarks at lower cost for most proprietary models.
