### [VISTA: Active Exploration for Self-Improvement in Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35051v1)

- **Authors:** Zhang Zhang, Shengjun Zhang, Simin Huang, Min Chen, Chong Xia, Xin Fei, Yueqi Duan
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35051v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35051v1)

Agentic embodied intelligence plays a foundational role in open-ended, dynamic physical environments. Rather than treating policy learning as an end-to-end mapping from instructions to actions, recent studies reveal that embodied systems depend not only on pre-trained foundation models, but also on harness. Prior approaches largely treat rollouts as static logs for post-hoc reflection, producing harness updates without autonomously testing and refining them through subsequent embodied interaction. To address this challenge, we propose VISTA, a meta-agent framework that transforms post-hoc rollout reflection into active self-exploration for embodied harness improvement. Specifically, VISTA maintains a harness graph that records the lineage of proposed harnesses, which enables the meta-agent to retrieve prior hypotheses, build on effective mechanisms, and avoid repeatedly exploring failed directions. Each iteration begins from a retained harness version and its execution evidence. Rather than directly committing an update from these historical trajectories, the meta-agent actively explores in the environment. It autonomously generates new rollouts, probes execution failures and recovery behaviors, and iteratively modifies the harness based on the resulting evidence. The refined harness is then evaluated afresh and added to the harness graph. We evaluate VISTA on the LIBERO-Pro, RoboCasa, and RoboTwin benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that VISTA progressively improves task success and is more rollout-efficient than static reflection, achieving higher success rates with fewer simulator rollouts.

### [The Target Agent Is Its Own Best Critic but a Poor Proposer: Self-Improved Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34965v1)

- **Authors:** Mintong Kang, Stephen Rawls, Melissa Kazemi Rad, Jian Ni, Bo Li
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.CR, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34965v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34965v1)

Automatic red teaming increasingly relies on large language models (LLMs) to iteratively diagnose failed attacks and propose improved adversarial prompts or injections. Although prior work strengthens this loop through prompt engineering and search-harness design, the mechanisms underlying its two core roles, criticism and attack proposal, has not been explored. To further investigate the core roles and therefore improve red teaming effectiveness, we disentangle two design choices: whether criticism is generated by the target session itself (self-criticism) or by a separately instantiated critic (external criticism), and whether that criticism is translated into the next attack by the target session (self-proposal) or by a separate attacker (external proposal). Through our analysis, we find that self-criticism is more effective because it is grounded in the target's live execution context, including observations, constraints, and available reasoning; removing reasoning reduces downstream attack success by 6%-17%. In contrast, self-proposal is substantially less effective because awareness of the adversarial objective induces explicit refusal and non-refusal sandbagging. These findings motivate a role-separated red-teaming approach: the target session diagnoses why an attack failed, while a separately instantiated attacker generates the next proposal. On DTAP-BENCH, spanning Telecom, Workflow, CRM, and Code domains, under both direct misuse and indirect threat models, as well as multiple agent frameworks, this configuration achieves 63.2% overall attack success rate (ASR), much higher than baselines. Our results show that effective automatic red teaming depends not only on model capability and search strategy, but also on how execution context and adversarial responsibility are distributed across model sessions.

### [How Do Coding-Agent Harnesses Self-Evolve? An Empirical Study of Retrospective Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34938v1)

- **Authors:** Christoph Treude, Sebastian Baltes
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.SE, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34938v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34938v1)

Coding agents can adapt their harnesses, the software, configuration, and instructions used to run them, based on experience from earlier tasks, but whether these adaptations improve their behavior on later tasks remains unclear. We study \retro, a skill that asks an agent to review a completed session and propose harness changes, and ask what changes it proposes and whether implemented changes reduce repeated work on later tasks. We ran Claude Code on 30 SWE-bench Verified issues, qualitatively coded the 125 proposals from \retro, implemented 24, and evaluated 12 harness changes on later issues from the same repositories in fresh sessions with and without each change. Most proposals referred to an event in the session, but 18 of 43 lint-related proposals recommended a practice regardless of what had happened. Among the seven evaluations in which sessions without the change encountered the targeted problem, two changes eliminated the targeted action, one reduced its occurrence, four showed no reduction, and some changes added work on unrelated issues. Harness self-evolution requires more than generating and implementing changes: it needs ways to establish whether a change helps on later tasks and to revise or remove changes that do not.

### [PhysEvolve: Verifier-grounded Self-evolution for Physical Reasoning with Executable Worlds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34921v1)

- **Authors:** Jinyuan Hu, Yilei Jiang, Chen Tang, Minghong Cai, Xinran Liu, Xiangyu Yue
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34921v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34921v1)

Physical reasoning underpins how embodied systems understand and act in the physical world, yet vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable on questions about dynamics and physical causality. Progress is critically constrained by the scarcity of grounded counterfactual training data, as observational videos capture only the factual trajectory that occurred. The self-evolving paradigm offers a route beyond fixed datasets by enabling models to construct their own training problems. Extending this paradigm to physics, however, requires an external source of truth that can both validate model-proposed environments and verify alternative physical outcomes under intervention. We introduce \physevolve{}, a verifier-grounded self-evolving framework that trains VLMs for causal physical reasoning from model-proposed executable worlds and simulation-authored tasks. At each round, a shared VLM proposes executable world programs and evidence schemas; a deterministic physics engine freezes each valid world, simulates matched baseline and body-elided counterfactual branches, and synthesizes discrete, answer-bound visual tasks. Exact verifier feedback from task solving updates both the shared policy via GRPO and an adaptive curriculum over physical intents. Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, \physevolve{} achieves targeted gains across PhysBench, CausalPhys, and ContPhy without human intervention data, improving PhysBench Throwing by $8.8$ points and ContPhy Counterfactual Removal and Goal-Pit by $7.4$ and $24.4$ points. These gains concentrate on interaction-sensitive and altered-world questions, demonstrating zero-shot transfer of causal principles to unseen complex dynamics (cloth, fluid, soft bodies) and real-world video.

### [RobotAgentBench: Towards Open-world Robot Autonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34649v1)

- **Authors:** Lingjun Mao, Jinglin Cao, Lukun He, Wenpeng Xu, Yuchen Yan, Hengyu Jin, Zijun Zhang, Jinzhou Tang, Songyao Jin, Ziche Liu, Yifei Shao, Junbo Huang, Fang Nan, Ruobing Han, Ziqiao Xi, Ziming Xu, Shuang Liang, Sibo Zhu, Wenyi Wu, Xinyue Wang, Yiming Huang, Xinkai Zou, Gao Zhu, Kun Zhou, Biwei Huang
- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.RO, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34649v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34649v1)

Recent advances in vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) have significantly improved robotic manipulation, yet deploying robots in the open world demands a higher level of autonomy beyond executing predefined tasks. Robot agents offer a promising paradigm for this transition, integrating task discovery, contextual reasoning, physical execution, and adaptive recovery into a closed interaction loop. However, existing benchmarks largely evaluate isolated capabilities, failing to capture the complexity of open-world robot autonomy. We introduce RobotAgentBench (RAB), a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating robot agents in realistic, open-world household environments. Through an agent-assisted, human-in-the-loop real-to-sim pipeline, RAB constructs 50 diverse household layouts with interactive objects and physically grounded tasks, encompassing 90 atomic tasks, 18 short-horizon tasks, 8 long-horizon tasks, and 4 open-ended scenarios. RAB provides a multi-level evaluation of robot agent capabilities: (1) task-level assessments systematically examine five complementary dimensions: task discovery, context reasoning, manipulation, failure recovery, and robustness; (2) scenario-level evaluation assesses how agents integrate these capabilities to autonomously fulfill high-level user needs. Furthermore, RAB enables systematic comparisons of standalone VLA/WAM models and diverse robot agent architectures under a unified planning-execution-verification framework. Extensive experiments reveal substantial gaps between manipulation and autonomy, demonstrate the advantages of agentic architectures and recursive self-improvement, and highlight the remaining challenges toward open-world robot autonomy.

### [ParetoRSI-Gym: Can AI Agents Improve Language Models Across Multiple Domains?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386v1)

- **Authors:** Haojin Wang, Yangzhen Wu, Zeyi Chen, Jian Xie, Jiaying Ye, Lili Meng, Shuyun Tang, Shangding Gu, Dawn Song
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34386v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34386v1)

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) requires an agentic system to design, train, and evaluate its own successor. Data-driven post-training is a central part of that loop, yet existing evaluations measure it on a single benchmark, where an agent selects from a static pool or distills a stronger model to push one score up in one run. Real post-training is multi-domain, and a gain in one domain has to be weighed against a regression in another. We propose ParetoRSI-Gym, in which an agent searches for data, curates it, trains, reads back per-domain results, and repeats, with everything but the data held fixed: the initial LLM, the five domains (math, code, finance, medicine, science), the toolset, and the SFT pipeline. Generalization benchmarks in each domain separate capability gains from overfitting to the scores the agent optimizes against. Across three agent systems, three base models, five rounds and multiple seeds, the strongest system, Claude Code with Opus-5, raises the generalization scores of its proxy-best checkpoint by 9.2% relative to the starting model (+2.3 absolute points), averaged with equal weight over three base models and five domains, with a single-domain relative gain of up to 63% (medicine on Ministral-3-3B). Further analysis shows that the harness matters as much as the model it drives, and that each agent keeps a unique data selection strategy. We see our work as a step towards evaluation of RSI systems in real-world machine learning engineering.

### [SEEK: Skill Extraction with Evolving Knowledge against Frontier Science Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34365v2)

- **Authors:** Cheng Gui, Xiang Zheng, Yutao Wu, Hanxun Huang, Yige Li, Kaidi Xu, Cong Wang, Wei-Ying Ma
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34365v2) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34365v2)

Science agents increasingly package domain expertise as reusable skills that plan analyses, select tools, and execute workflows. This modularity creates a confidentiality risk: the interactions that deliver a skill's capability can also reveal the skill itself. We build an extensible benchmark of 40 protected skills across eight open- and closed-source science-agent platforms, comparing direct, fixed-strategy, and bandit-guided iterative probing under reference-based and reference-free evaluation. Leakage is pervasive: all eight platforms (100%) leak at least one protected skill, and four reach 100% attack success under a single direct request. To stress-test platforms that resist simple probing, we propose SEEK (Skill Extraction with Evolving Knowledge), a black-box, self-evolving red-teaming agent that consolidates response-derived evidence in persistent memory and evolves reusable attack skills, using only user-visible responses. On 12 skills from SpatialAgent and Biomni, SEEK achieves 100% attack success while reducing the mean first-success round by 31.8% and 38.2% relative to bandit-guided probing. Against a confidentiality policy that blocks all three benchmark protocols, SEEK still extracts one of six protected skills. These findings show that current science agents do not reliably protect their skills and that defenses must be evaluated against adaptive, multi-turn attackers.

### [Self-Evolution Reasoner: Continuous Optimization via Policy-Intrinsic Exploration and Exploitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34137v1)

- **Authors:** Wenhang Shi, Yiren Chen, Zhe Zhao, Jinhao Dong, Wei Lu, Xiaoyong Du
- **Published:** 2026-10-25
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34137v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34137v1)

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) enables large language models (LLMs) to continuously self-improve their reasoning capabilities. Compared to RLVR's scalar rewards, recent self-supervised on-policy distillation methods leverage denser supervision signals. However, both paradigms suffer from exploration bottlenecks, struggling to escape local optima when the policy fails to sample valid solutions. Relying on external demonstrations can bypass this issue; however, the off-policy nature hinders genuine self-improvement and the mismatched-distribution reasoning can not be fully exploited. In this work, we propose Self-Evolution Policy Optimization (SEPO), a framework that drives continuous reasoning capability evolution via guided exploration and in-distribution exploitation. Specifically, SEPO utilizes labels to elicit correct reasoning from the policy, unlocking the exploration potential. By treating current policy conditioned on this in-distribution reasoning as a self-teacher, SEPO distills the newly discovered solution back into the policy. Through this iterative cycle of policy-intrinsic exploration and exploitation, SEPO achieves robust and continuous capability evolution. Across scientific, tool-use, and complex logical reasoning domains, SEPO consistently achieves the best performance under identical training time or steps, demonstrating superior efficiency and convergence ceilings. Notably, SEPO maintains stable improvements even in scenarios where baselines severely underperform or fail entirely, such as on complex tasks or with small-scale models. Our code is available at https://github.com/Eric8932/SEPO.

### [WebViSE: Scaling Visual Design Intelligence from the Open Web for LLM Website Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33929v1)

- **Authors:** Yifei Sun, Peng Zhao, Le Zhang, Bingjin Chen, Zhengfan Wu
- **Published:** 2026-10-24
- **Priority:** —
- **Venue:** arXiv
- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33929v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33929v1)

Large language models have made remarkable strides in website development, enabling complete and interactive web experiences to be built from natural-language instructions with far less effort. Yet, non-expert users often struggle to articulate sophisticated visual and interaction designs in natural language. Meanwhile, the open web already contains a vast reservoir of such design knowledge, embodied in mature website implementations. To this end, we introduce Website-derived Visual Skill Extraction (WebViSE), a framework that automatically abstracts these implementations into reusable Visual Skills and builds a structured Skill library. To scale the library, WebViSE organizes its Skills in a multi-parent DAG and jointly evolves Skill descriptions and DAG structure using Skill-conditioned Potential Queries, enabling efficient matching between diverse user instructions and relevant Skills. The auto-evolved structure outperforms non-hierarchical LLM retrieval baselines at only 39.09% of the estimated retrieval cost with Qwen3.8-Max. For any models, WebViSE can be directly injected as plug-and-play guidance, enabling diverse models to produce more visually compelling and aesthetically refined web pages. For open-source models, WebViSE can be used to build a data-synthesis pipeline that internalizes visual design intelligence into model weights. Experiments across multiple web development benchmarks and human evaluation consistently show that WebViSE improves the visual quality and interaction fidelity of artifacts. Overall, our work establishes an automated path for extracting, organizing, and transferring design intelligence from the open web to website-building models. We release all extracted Visual Skills, the associated Skill DAG, and our code at https://github.com/YifeiSun-0710/WebViSE.

### [Build, Don't Play: Self-Improving LLM Research Agents Develop a Hearthstone Battlegrounds AI That Rivals Top Players](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33845v1)

### [BellowsKV: One Latent for Many Layers, Expanded on Demand](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35023v1)

Large language models (LLMs) now serve as the backbone of agents that reason over many turns, call tools, and pursue long-horizon tasks. To avoid recomputation, a model stores the attention keys and values of all past tokens in a key-value (KV) cache. Each layer maintains its own cache, so KV-cache memory can become the main bottleneck for GPU serving. Most compression methods operate within a single layer, by quantizing the cache entries, reducing the channel dimension, or evicting less important tokens. All three lose substantial accuracy at high compression ratios. Cross-layer methods share one compressed cache across adjacent layers to go beyond this limit. However, these methods also degrade sharply at high compression ratios. We find that the KV-cache representations of adjacent layers are quite different, whereas the input hidden states of adjacent layers are highly similar. We also find that, under the same memory budget, reducing the dimension of this shared latent loses far more accuracy than quantizing it. At inference time, each layer reads its own keys and values from this latent through a layer-specific projection. We keep the latent at full dimension and compress it in two other ways: BellowsKV-Quant stores the latent at low precision, which is nearly lossless because the latent has no dominant outlier channels; BellowsKV-Evict makes one shared eviction decision per block, because the layers rank token importance very similarly. Across Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, BellowsKV-Quant retains about 99% of full-cache accuracy at 3.6x compression and 97-98% at 13-14x, where the strongest cross-layer method retains at most 84% at 16x. ### [What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491v1)

- **Affiliations:** n/a
- **Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Links:** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34491v1)

Generative models increasingly expose a reasoning effort setting that lets users scale inference-time compute, trading higher cost for potentially improved performance. Users must choose an effort level before knowing whether extra reasoning will help, and even a successful run does not reveal whether the effort purchased was necessary. Reasoning effort is therefore a credence good: buyers observe the outcome but cannot assess the marginal value of the computation they paid for. In this work, we measure the counterfactual effect of effort levels on both proprietary and open-weight models on four benchmarks, spanning single-call reasoning and long-horizon agentic tasks. These findings show that naively choosing the highest level is not only cost-inefficient but also offers no guarantee of the best instance-level performance. A simple alternative, starting at the lowest effort and escalating only after a verified failure, matches or improves on fixed top effort across all models and benchmarks at lower cost for most proprietary models.
